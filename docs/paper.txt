SECURITY | DATA BREACHES
pCloud Says Attack Exposed User Emails

pCloud said the attack on its pCloud Pass password manager exposed a small number of user email addresses and triggered login attempts against those accounts.

*Why it matters:* Customers trust the Swiss company with every credential they own, and users reported unknown devices connecting even with two-factor authentication turned on.

*What they're saying:* pCloud said its team promptly contained the attack, responding to user reports that surfaced on Reddit.

*What to do:* Review connected devices and active sessions in pCloud Pass, remove any device you do not recognize, and change the account password.

Sources: The Hacker News, BleepingComputer

SECURITY | VULNERABILITIES AND EXPLOITS
Researchers Find God Mode Visa Flaws

Researchers disclosed god mode vulnerabilities in the online visa and immigration systems of Nigeria, Ethiopia, Zambia, Sri Lanka, Fiji, and Ghana.

*Why it matters:* The flaws gave attackers a path to disrupt traveler entry at six national borders and to reach the applicant data behind those portals.

*The big picture:* Border control runs on internet-facing web applications. One broken permission check in a visa portal reaches the same records an immigration officer sees.

*What to do:* Audit administrative access paths in citizen-facing government portals and test for privilege escalation that hands ordinary users full system control.

Sources: Dark Reading

WeaveLotus Spies on Southeast Asian Governments

SOCRadar attributed a previously undocumented campaign it calls WeaveLotus to a Vietnam-sponsored group hitting military, government, and legislative targets across Southeast Asia.

*Why it matters:* The operators hunted intelligence on South China Sea defense and military agreements, adding a new state sponsor to the region's espionage map.

*By the numbers:* SOCRadar counted five intrusions in the campaign, including activity in Thailand.

*What to watch:* Defense ministries and legislatures in the region sit squarely in scope as South China Sea negotiations continue.

Sources: The Hacker News

SECURITY | DATA BREACHES
Japan Education Ministry Discloses Procurement Breach

Japan's education ministry said hackers breached its government procurement system, potentially exposing records that hold business information and email addresses.

*Why it matters:* Procurement platforms carry supplier and contract data that attackers reuse for targeted invoice fraud and phishing against government vendors.

*What to do:* Suppliers that bid through the system should treat unexpected payment or contract emails as suspect and verify requests by phone.
