1. ConnectWise Suspends Cloud Services

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [breach, credentials]

Latest developments: Sophos detailed on October 19, 2026 how automated sign-ins used ConnectWise's Entra application identity to request Microsoft Graph tokens across every tenant that had consented to it, a cross-tenant token sweep; ConnectWise kept Cloud Backup, the service it formerly sold as SkyKick, along with SaaS Security and Cloud Management offline through remediation and security validation, and says customer impact remains unconfirmed.

read more

Attackers used a compromised credential belonging to the ConnectWise Cloud Backup application identity to sign in to customer Microsoft 365 tenants, 173 of them by researchers' count. Managed service providers and their customers should audit Microsoft 365 sign-in logs and application consent grants for ConnectWise identities and arrange interim backups while the services stay down.

2. NGINX, Plex, and FortiGate Under Fire

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, zero-day, exploit]

Latest developments: Unit 42 updated its threat brief on October 19, 2026 to say the ransomware operators it tracks as Spikey Scorpius, known publicly as Qilin, and Tarnished Scorpius, known as Inc, now exploit PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257; F5 broke its schedule the same day and shipped out-of-band patches for several NGINX web server flaws, three of them critical bugs that run an attacker's code; a researcher using the alias boredhackerblog found attackers chaining four Plex Media Server flaws, the set called Plex2Shell, to drop cryptocurrency miners and reverse proxy software a day after the proof of concept went public; and researchers linked the already-exploited Fortinet flaw to a stealthy, persistent implant one intruder planted on a FortiGate firewall at a telecommunications company.

read more

All four products sit at the network edge, where NGINX fronts much of the web, Plex servers face the internet by design, and PAN-OS and FortiGate firewalls terminate remote access. Patch NGINX, Plex, and PAN-OS now, and hunt FortiGate devices for the implant and for deleted logs; a clean firmware version proves nothing by itself.

3. Poisoned Plugins and Packages Spread Backdoors

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [supply chain, backdoor, wordpress]

Latest developments: Rank Math said on October 16, 2026 that copies of Rank Math PRO version 3.0.123 downloaded from rankmath.com or delivered as updates between 20:02 UTC on October 15 and 07:12 UTC on October 16 carried a self-restoring backdoor; OpenSourceMalware found the GhostAction crew now phishing open-source developers and hiding a credential stealer inside a fake font file across 94 GitHub repositories; and Elastic traced a fake Web3 accelerator that offers free Anthropic Claude and Google Gemini credits and then ships information stealers and backdoors to Windows and macOS through malicious npm packages.

read more

Rank Math PRO is the paid edition of a widely installed WordPress search engine optimization plugin, and its own update channel delivered the malware. Treat any site that installed or activated one of those copies as compromised, rotate its credentials, hunt the backdoor that reinstalls itself, and tell developers that npm packages promising free AI credits are bait.

4. Ad Bidding Delivers Spyware as Pegasus Hits Serbia

Nation-State Activity · [spyware, zero-click]

Latest developments: Dark Reading named the technique AdFection on October 19, 2026: spyware vendors buy targeted impressions in the digital ad auction to fingerprint and track individuals and organizations of interest, then infect the selected handset the moment it renders the ad; researchers found Pegasus on the phone of the rector of the University of Belgrade, the latest in a run of such discoveries among Serbian opposition figures ahead of national elections; and a federal judge denied WhatsApp's bid to hold NSO Group in contempt of a 2025 injunction barring it from using the messaging app to spread spyware, saying WhatsApp hurt its own case by withholding information.

read more

Mercenary spyware makers now reach a target through the same real-time advertising auctions that serve ordinary apps and websites, which requires no click and leaves little trace. Journalists, politicians, and civil society staff should expect zero-click delivery through ads, and the organizations that employ them should block ads and trackers on managed phones and run periodic forensic checks.
