1. CRYPTO WALLET PLATFORMS UNDER LIVE ATTACK
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [cryptocurrency, exploit]
   Latest developments: Keplr and Web3Auth said late last week
   that an attacker exploited a flaw at Web3Auth, which runs
   Keplr's Google and Apple sign-in, to steal from wallets,
   after which Web3Auth took the attacker's own route and
   emptied other exposed wallets first; the LNbits project
   separately told every operator on November 8, 2026 to shut
   down their Bitcoin Lightning wallet servers while it prepares
   fixes, after new accounts used an exploit to withdraw
   2,285,545 satoshis, about 0.023 bitcoin, from one operator's
   server.
   Keplr is a cryptocurrency wallet that outsources social login
   to Web3Auth, so a flaw at the login provider reached
   customers' keys. LNbits is free, open-source software for
   running Bitcoin Lightning wallets; owners who signed into
   Keplr with Google or Apple should move their funds, and
   LNbits operators should keep servers down until the project
   ships its patches.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/web3auth-flaw-exploited-to-steal-keplr.html
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/lnbits-urges-all-operators-to-shut-down.html

2. AMPLIFON AND FRONTLINE BREACH TALLIES GROW
   Data Breaches · [breach, healthcare, education]
   Latest developments: Amplifon Americas disclosed that hackers
   took personal, health, and insurance information, Social

   internal systems, and Frontline Education told Indiana
   regulators that its August 2026 breach reached more than 3

   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/3-million-people-hit-by-frontline-education-data-breach/

3. DEEPMIND AGENTS CHEAPEN ATTACKS ON RSA-1024
   AI Security · [ai, cryptography]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek reported on November 9,
   2026 that Google DeepMind's AI agents, working alongside
   cryptographers, cut the cost of forging RSA-1024 signatures
   and found a cheaper method for factoring 1024-bit keys.
   RSA-1024 keys still sign and encrypt traffic across legacy
   applications, embedded devices, and older certificate chains.
   Any organization running them should inventory where they sit
   and retire them in favor of 2048-bit keys or larger.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/google-deepminds-ai-agents-make-1024-bit-rsa-keys-cheaper-to-attack/

4. CRÉDIT MUTUEL ARKÉA'S MILLIONS HEAD TO CRYPTO
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [fraud, banking]
   Latest developments: Help Net Security reported on November
   9, 2026 that the attackers wired an estimated $133 million
   out of Crédit Mutuel Arkéa's own account to other banks,
   where someone possibly converted it into cryptocurrency, and
   that the blockchain investigator ZachXBT began tracking more
   than $100 million in suspected stolen funds leaving
   centralized exchange accounts days before the bank confirmed
   the attack.
   Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, the French cooperative banking group
   with 211.5 billion euros in total assets and more than 5.8
   million customers, traces the intrusion to a compromised
   service provider and has suspended certain systems. Banks
   that let suppliers touch payment infrastructure should
   re-examine what those accounts can move and how fast.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/09/credit-mutuel-arkea-cyberattack-133-million/

5. AI AGENTS LIFT TAIPEI EMERGENCY DISPATCH RECORDS
   AI Security · [ai, breach]
   Latest developments: Acronis's Threat Research Unit reported
   last week, in coverage published November 9, 2026, that an
   attacker in Taiwan used BOSSAI agents to pull emergency
   dispatch records covering seven Taipei districts, and that
   the same agents turned up a Taipei fire department platform
   serving 2018 patient data, electrocardiogram readings and
   ambulance video footage included, to anyone who asked, with
   no login.
   BOSSAI is an AI agent framework, and the stolen records
   document emergency medical calls the Taipei fire department
   handled. The researchers hold high confidence that one person
   ran the entire operation, which shows how far agent tooling
   compresses the headcount an intrusion once required.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/taiwan-based-hacker-used-bossai-ai.html

6. HOMELAND SECURITY INVESTIGATIONS' SUBPOENA SURGE
   Policy and Regulation · [policy, privacy, surveillance]
   Latest developments: Wired reported on November 9, 2026 that
   Homeland Security Investigations estimates it has issued
   roughly 185,000 administrative subpoenas since January 2025,
   more than the unit sent across the six and a half years from
   2016 to mid-August 2022.
   Administrative subpoenas let Homeland Security Investigations
   demand customer records from a company without a judge's
   sign-off, which leaves each recipient to judge the demand on
   its own. Companies holding subscriber, payment, or location
   data should route these requests through counsel and keep a
   log of every one.
   - Wired Security: https://www.wired.com/story/dhs-has-used-this-controversial-investigative-tool-185000-times-since-trump-took-office/
