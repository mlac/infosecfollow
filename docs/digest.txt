1. CHECK POINT GATEWAYS FALL TO PHP2SHELL AND A GAIA OS BUG
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch, apt]
   Latest developments: Check Point shipped emergency hotfixes
   on November 13, 2026 for a high-severity flaw attackers have
   exploited since late October to take root and hold
   persistence, and the company detailed a campaign that started
   October 30, 2026 against Israeli organizations: an
   unidentified actor entered unpatched Security Gateways
   through the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities, escalated to root with
   CVE-2026-123039, a previously unreported local privilege
   escalation in Gaia OS, installed a backdoor named TSGA, and
   ran data-wiping malware against connected VMware ESXi hosts,
   with a wider wave following; Citrix separately closed 10
   critical NetScaler vulnerabilities that drive unexpected
   behavior and denial of service.
   Check Point Security Gateways sit at the perimeter of
   enterprise networks and route all traffic through Gaia OS.
   Administrators should install the hotfixes immediately, hunt
   for TSGA, and check ESXi datastores for deleted files.

   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, windows]
   Latest developments: Microsoft confirmed on November 13, 2026
   that the November 2026 Patch Tuesday updates fail Windows
   activation and licensing with 0x80041008 errors, and warned
   earlier in the week that the same updates may break printing
   of documents carrying complex formatting; researchers

7. CLICKFIX LURES WEAR ZILLOW, TEAMS, AND AI BRANDS
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [phishing, malware, social
   engineering]
   Latest developments: The SANS Internet Storm Center
   documented Macfinger, a ClickFix campaign that pushes the
   DownTheDRAIN infostealer onto macOS, which extends the
   pattern three other teams reported this week: EfficientIP
   Research Labs traced ClickFix pages dressed as Zillow
   apartment reviews that copied a PowerShell command to the
   visitor's clipboard behind a routine-looking verification
   check and installed NetSupport RAT with its window hidden and
   a restart at every login; the browser security company Island
   counted 186 lookalike domains impersonating AI tools, a
   pattern it calls TypoPrompting, serving the same verification
   lures plus browser-hijacking malware and search-hijacking
   extensions; and Huntress documented attackers who used a fake
   Microsoft Teams meeting scheduler to talk an employee at a
   construction company into typing a device code into
   Microsoft's genuine sign-in page, which handed them a valid
   session token before the Huntress security operations center
   caught it.
   All of these campaigns ask the victim to perform one
   normal-looking step—paste a command, enter a code—and that
   step delivers remote access, an infostealer, or a session
   token. Blocking clipboard-to-shell execution on Windows and
   macOS, disabling the device code sign-in flow where business
   needs allow, and filtering lookalike AI domains cut off the
   common path.

   Nation-State Activity · [apt, telecom, espionage]
   Latest developments: Enea, the Stockholm telecommunications
