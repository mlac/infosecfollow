1. CAIRN AGENTS ROOT A MONAI LABEL SERVER
   AI Security · [ai, zero-day, apt]
   Amazon Threat Intelligence named the target software:
   autonomous Cairn agents found an unauthenticated file-write
   flaw in MONAI Label and took root on a university server.
   Why it matters: An AI framework discovered and weaponized an
   unknown bug in one run, erasing the window defenders rely on
   between disclosure and exploitation.
   - What to do: Audit MONAI Label installs at version 0.8.5 or
     earlier, which ship exploitable in the default setup, and
     take the open-source imaging server off the internet.
   - Zoom in: The flaw lets an attacker with no account write
     files outside the server's storage folder, which the agents
     turned into root access.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/amazon-china-linked-hackers-ai-agents-exploited-medical-software-zero-day/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-ai-agents-found-zero-day-in.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-use-autonomous-ai-tool-cairn-to-find-zero-day-take-over-server/
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/amazon-research-cairn-ai-university-attacks-possible-zero-day

2. GNUPG AND WHATSAPP PATCH CRITICAL FLAWS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, vulnerability]
   GnuPG warned that a malicious OpenPGP key with an unusually
   long user ID triggers a critical memory bug in GPGME, tracked
   as CVE-2026-129975, and shipped fixes.
   Why it matters: Every application built on GPGME, the
   standard library programs use to work with GnuPG, can leak
   its data to anyone who supplies a crafted key.
   - What to do: Move to GPGME 2.3.0 or later. Android users
     need WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business 2.26.17.4 or newer to
     close CVE-2026-23871.
   - Between the lines: WhatsApp's security team found the
     ad-reply URL validation bug while disrupting a scam
     campaign and suspects attackers used it to make scam
     messages look credible.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/gnupg-warns-critical-gpgme-flaw-could.html
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/whatsapp-patches-3-security-flaws.html
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/whatsapp-fixes-flaw-uncovered-while-disrupting-scam-campaign/

3. MALICIOUS WEBPAGE HIJACKS META MUSE CHATS
   AI Security · [ai, privacy]
   Researchers showed a single malicious webpage pulling a Meta
   Muse user's private conversations and stored memories out of
   the assistant with no approval prompt.
   Why it matters: The same attack reaches credentials Muse
   holds for a user's connected accounts, turning one assistant
   into a silent path to every linked service.
   - Zoom in: Signal will restrict screenshots by default on
     Android and iOS to stop AI agents from reading chats, and
     calls the protection incomplete.
   - By the numbers: A researcher planted 2 million tracking
     canaries in prompts across the AI supply chain and watched
     secret user data escape at a high rate.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/a-single-malicious-webpage-let.html
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/signal-blocks-ai-agents-from-reading.html
   - Ars Technica Security: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/

4. CISA FLAGS SAP NETWEAVER AND VCENTER EXPLOITS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, zero-day, apt]
   CISA added four actively exploited flaws to its Known
   Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Friday, covering Apache
   Roller, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver, and Broadcom VMware
   vCenter.
   Why it matters: SAP NetWeaver joins the exploited list,
   pushing the current espionage campaign into enterprise
   resource planning estates that run finance and logistics.
   - What to do: Patch all four products on CISA's federal
     timeline. The Apache Roller deserialization bug,
     CVE-2026-129448, carries a 9.8 severity score and needs no
     credentials.
   - What to watch: Dark Reading reported that China-nexus group
     Armory Castle likely held its next vCenter zero-day ready
     during the last round of VMware updates.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-critical-vmware-cisco.html
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/china-armory-castle-vmware-vcenter-zero-day

5. ATTACKERS EXPLOIT ENTRA CONNECT HEALTH FLAW
   Data Breaches · [breach, vulnerability, identity]
   Microsoft said attackers exploited a vulnerability in its
   Entra Connect Health service and took administrator email
   addresses and tenant configuration information.
   Why it matters: Tenant configuration data maps an
   organization's identity setup, handing attackers a targeting
   list for follow-on phishing against named administrators.
   - What to do: Treat affected administrator addresses as
     public and brief those accounts on targeted phishing that
     cites Microsoft identity services.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-entra-connect-health-flaw-exploited-to-steal-tenant-data/

6. RACKSPACE SPOT BREACH EXPOSES KUBERNETES SECRETS
   Data Breaches · [breach, cloud]
   Rackspace told customers that attackers used compromised
   credentials to breach its Spot cloud platform, deploy
   unauthorized workloads, and read Kubernetes secrets in
   customer clusters.
   Why it matters: Kubernetes secrets hold the keys and tokens
   customer applications run on, so one platform breach carries
   attackers into every system those keys unlock.
   - What to do: Rotate every secret stored in Rackspace Spot
     clusters and review those clusters for workloads nobody on
     the team authorized.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/rackspace-spot-breach-let-hackers-access-customer-kubernetes-secrets/

7. FBI'S NATIONAL CRIME DATABASE REPORTEDLY BREACHED
   Data Breaches · [breach, government]
   Attackers breached the FBI's National Crime Information
   Center, the nationwide police database, in the agency's
   second reported intrusion in two months.
   Why it matters: NCIC carries records that local, state, and
   federal agencies share daily, so stolen data would reach well
   past the FBI's own systems.
   - Yes, but: The account comes from reporting rather than an
     FBI statement. The bureau has not publicly described the
     intrusion or its scope.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fbi-reportedly-hacked-again-this-time-targeting-nationwide-police-database/

8. BADBOX BOTNET RETURNS AT RECORD SCALE
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [botnet, fraud, malware]
   The BADBOX malware found in generic Android TV boxes returned
   to large-scale ad fraud, driving a record 40 billion ad
   requests a day.
   Why it matters: Advertisers bankroll the operation through
   fraudulent impressions, and every infected box sits inside a
   home or office network as attacker infrastructure.
   - Flashback: Takedowns disrupted BADBOX last year. The
     operators rebuilt the botnet and now run it past its
     earlier volume.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/badbox-30-botnet-returns-with-record-breaking-40-billion-daily-ad-requests/

   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [ransomware, fraud]

   co-founder of ransomwarehelp.com with wire fraud after an FBI
