2. ATTACKERS TAKE COMMANDS ON UNREALIRCD SERVERS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, exploit]
   Latest developments: CISA added CVE-2026-114713 to its Known
   Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Friday, November 13,
   2026 after reports of active exploitation, and the flaw's
   mechanism came into focus: improper neutralization of
   delimiters, rated 8.6, hands an attacker command injection at
   the server level.
   UnrealIRCd runs open-source Internet Relay Chat servers, many
   of them on long-lived Linux hosts that administrators rarely
   revisit. Operators should upgrade now, and federal civilian
   agencies face a remediation deadline under the KEV listing.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-exploit-unrealircd-flaw-that.html

3. IMPLANTS RIDE TREZOR SAFE 5 WALLETS FROM A RESELLER
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [supply chain, crypto]
   Latest developments: Trezor confirmed that three Safe 5
   devices sold by CryptoBilis, a Southeast Asian reseller,
   carry an extra circuit board that reads the wallet's recovery
   phrase off its screen and sends the phrase over the cellular
   network to a server the attackers control, and the company
   released a fix.
   Trezor examined one of the wallets in its lab and identified
   the other two. Anyone holding a Safe 5 bought through
   CryptoBilis should apply Trezor's fix and move funds to a new
   recovery phrase generated on a device bought direct.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/trezor-confirms-crypto-stealing-implants-in-three-wallets-releases-fix/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/trezor-confirms-hidden-hardware.html

4. ARCTICRAT SCORES VICTIMS WHILE A FAKE GTA 6 DROPS WARDEN
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, mobile, crypto]
   Latest developments: ArcticRAT, the Android malware, has

5. MIOR AND SKYFALL CRACK AMD SEV-SNP
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [cloud, hardware]
   Latest developments: Researchers published two attacks this
   week, Mior and Skyfall, that let the operator of a server
   read data inside AMD SEV-SNP virtual machines, with Mior also
   modifying a guest's memory and forging the signed attestation
   report that tells a customer which software the machine runs.
   AMD SEV-SNP underpins confidential computing at the major
   clouds, where it promises that the host cannot see a tenant's
   data. Both attacks require control of the hypervisor and Mior
   also requires control of the BIOS, which places the risk on
   malicious or compromised hosting providers.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/new-mior-and-skyfall-attacks-let-rogue.html

6. NEW YORK CITY BARS SURVEILLANCE PRICING
   Policy and Regulation · [policy, privacy]
   Latest developments: The New York City Council passed a bill
   that lets the city fine a company $1,000 for each verified
   instance of surveillance pricing, the practice of setting a
   shopper's price from that shopper's personal data, as
   comparable bans gain ground in other jurisdictions.
   Retailers and platforms that tune prices to an individual's
   browsing history, location, or device now face per-instance
   penalties in the largest city in the United States. Companies
   selling into New York should audit what personal signals
   their pricing engines consume.
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/nyc-council-passes-bill-ban-surveillance-pricing
