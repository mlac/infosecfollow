1. BEYONDTRUST ZERO-DAY OPENS REMOTE SUPPORT SERVERS
   Nation-State Activity · [zero-day, apt, patch]
   Latest developments: CISA added CVE-2026-102764 to its Known
   Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 16, 2026 and
   told organizations running BeyondTrust Remote Support 26.2.3
   and earlier to patch at once, upgrading first if they sit
   below version 24.3.1, saying attackers use the 9.3-rated
   improper origin validation flaw for initial access and then
   establish persistence, escalate privileges, and move
   laterally; researchers tie the zero-day exploitation to
   Armory Castle, the China-nexus espionage group they
   previously tracked as UNC5221 and Warp Panda, which broke in
   during late August 2026, installed custom tools, and deleted
   logs and artifacts to cover its path.
   BeyondTrust Remote Support, the product organizations once
   bought as Bomgar, gives help desks privileged access to
   employee machines, so a takeover of the server hands an
   attacker that same reach. Administrators running Remote
   Support should apply the fix and hunt for deleted logs and
   unexplained sessions dating to August 2026.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/chinese-apt-exploited-beyondtrust-vulnerability-as-zero-day/

   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, malware, zero-day]
   Latest developments: CISA added CVE-2025-61624 to its Known
   Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 16, 2026, the
   path traversal flaw in the command line interpreter of
   FortiOS, FortiPAM, FortiProxy, and FortiSwitchManager that a
   suspected advanced actor exploits to deliver PEERSTITCH, a

   should patch FortiOS, F5, Juniper, and Office now, and
   Windows 11 users who cannot enable Smart App Control after
   October's update should treat the control as off.

6. BLOCKSTRAP AND OPENBSD HAND OVER ROOT
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [rce, patch, exploit]
   Latest developments: Researchers warned on October 16, 2026
   that attackers may soon exploit CVE-2026-103849, a
   maximum-severity flaw in the BlockStrap component of Magento
   and Adobe Commerce that runs arbitrary PHP code on a store's
   server, and the security firm Calif published a full proof of
   concept that takes root on OpenBSD 7.9 by chaining a roughly
   37-year-old integer overflow in shared memory with a separate
   kernel flaw, defeating OpenBSD's KARL kernel address
   randomization along with SMAP and SMEP hardware protections.
   A BlockStrap takeover gives an attacker the code that handles
   every checkout on an online store, and the OpenBSD chain
   turns any local account on a patched-reputation operating
   system into root. Merchants should patch Magento and Adobe
   Commerce now, and OpenBSD administrators should install the
   fixes the project shipped before Calif released the exploit.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/critical-blockstrap-flaw-lets-hackers-run-code-on-magento-servers/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/researchers-demonstrate-openbsd-root.html

8. BOTNETS DRIVE 9.1 MILLION DDOS ATTACKS
   Critical Infrastructure Security · [ddos, botnet]
   Latest developments: Netscout counted more than 9.1 million
   distributed denial-of-service attacks worldwide between
   January and June 2026, up nearly a quarter year over year,
   with attacks above 1 terabit per second or 1 billion packets
   per second climbing 1,286%, growth the firm traces to the
   botnets Aisuru, Kimwolf, and Jackskid and their successors,

   resells the victims' bandwidth through a commercial
   residential proxy network. Owners of edge routers and access
   points should update firmware and close remote
   administration, and service operators should size defenses
   for packet rates rather than bandwidth alone.
