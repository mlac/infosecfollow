1. OPTIMIZELY SCRIPTS SERVE MALWARE TO 178 SITES
   Software Supply Chain Security · [supply-chain, skimmer,
   exploit]
   Latest developments: Optimizely confirmed early Tuesday,
   November 17, 2026 that an unauthorized party exploited a
   vulnerability in its Experimentation product and added
   malicious JavaScript to some customer projects, and Report
   URI, which monitors the scripts websites run, counted at
   least 178 organizations serving that code to visitors on
   Monday, among them CNN, the BBC, Fox News, Zoom, GitLab,
   Salesforce, Equifax, HSBC, and IKEA.
   Customers embed Optimizely Web through a JavaScript snippet
   their pages fetch from Optimizely's own servers, so tampering
   at Optimizely reaches every visitor. The injected code stole
   cryptocurrency and payment card details typed on checkout
   pages and fired exploit kits at iOS devices; site owners
   should audit Monday's sessions and card transactions and
   force users to rotate exposed wallet credentials.

2. AI AGENTS RUN INTRUSIONS WITH NO HUMAN
   AI Security · [ai, apt, ransomware]
   Latest developments: Google Threat Intelligence Group and

4. SHADOWCLOUD HIDES C2 IN GITHUB AND SHAREPOINT
   Nation-State Activity · [apt, espionage, malware]
   Latest developments: Kaspersky published both halves of its
   analysis of Operation ShadowCloud, a campaign it first
   identified in early 2026 against government networks in Asia
   and Africa, naming two previously undocumented
   backdoors—GitDoor, which takes orders through GitHub, and
   GraphDoor, which runs its command and control through
   Microsoft SharePoint—plus the shellcode families and plug-ins
   they load; Unit 42 disclosed a separate Southeast Asian
   espionage campaign built on the PerfectPioneer remote access
   trojan and the modular KUKURAT, which hide their traffic in

   services a government network already trusts, which blunts
   domain blocking and reputation filtering. Defenders in
   government environments should inspect outbound HTTP/3 and
   QUIC traffic and treat machine-to-machine calls to GitHub and
   SharePoint as worth logging.

5. ANTHROPIC CUTS DISCLOSURE TO 30 DAYS
   Policy and Regulation · [policy, disclosure, patch]
   Latest developments: Anthropic will publish high- and
   critical-severity vulnerabilities 30 days after it reports
   them to a vendor or an open-source maintainer, down from 90,
   and give every other bug 45 days, with the new deadlines
   covering reports it sends on or after January 1, 2027 and
   already-open reports keeping their original clocks; Dark
   Reading found practitioners split over Google Project Zero's
   parallel move from 120 days to 30 over the next 14 months.
   Anthropic argues a reported bug can no longer stay private
   for months, because one model finding a flaw implies others
   will find it too. Vendors and maintainers who depend on long
   remediation windows have six weeks to rebuild their patch
   pipelines before the shorter deadlines take effect.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/17/anthropic-vulnerability-disclosure-days/

   AI Security · [ai, agents, governance]

   Policy and Regulation · [policy, encryption, quantum]
   Latest developments: The National Institute of Standards and
   Technology finalized its post-quantum cryptography guidance,
