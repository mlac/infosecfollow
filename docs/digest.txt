1. A COMMAND POLICY FOR AI CODING AGENTS
   AI Security · [ai, identity]
   Latest developments: Help Net Security's November 8, 2026
   roundup carries an interview in which Vinay Thakker, chief
   technology officer of KloudStax, lays out the first ten lines
   of a command policy for AI coding agents: each agent runs
   under its own identity, carries a person's identity only
   while acting for that person, and operates inside set
   credential and network limits.
   Development teams now hand coding agents shell access,
   repositories, and cloud credentials with no identity of their
   own, which blurs who performed an action. Thakker's rules tie
   every agent command to a distinct identity, so reviewers can
   separate agent activity from human activity in the logs.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/08/week-in-review-refrigeration-cyberattacks-hit-european-retailers-again-patch-tuesday-forecast/

3. DIGICERT BREACH REACHES BUSINESS CUSTOMER RECORDS
   Data Breaches · [breach, phishing]
   Latest developments: The Record and SecurityWeek reported on
   November 6, 2026 that the DigiCert exposure ran through the
   summer and that an outsider created an unauthorized account
   on the internal business system reachable from the internet,
   then took customer names, email addresses, phone numbers, and
   business addresses.
   DigiCert issues the TLS certificates that secure a large
   share of the web, and the stolen records amount to a vetted
   contact list for its corporate customers. Those customers
   should treat mail citing their real account details as
   suspect and verify any certificate request through a known
   channel.
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/digicert-certificate-authority-reports-data-breach
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/digicert-data-breach-impacts-business-customer-information/

4. AIRPLANE SOUNDS PINPOINT WHERE A VIDEO WAS SHOT
   Privacy and Surveillance · [privacy, osint]
   Latest developments: Bruce Schneier flagged on November 6,
   2026 a technique that places an otherwise unidentifiable
   recording by hunting its audio for the faint sound of passing
   aircraft and matching the resulting Doppler curves to the
   paths planes flew overhead.
   Investigators, journalists, and companies that film inside
   their own facilities now leak location through ambient sound
   as well as image metadata, and should mask or strip audio
   before publishing. BleepingComputer separately spelled out
   how the IMSI and IMEI identifiers a phone broadcasts to
   networks let a nearby collector track the handset.
   - Schneier on Security: https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/11/pinpointing-location-of-videos-from-background-airplane-sounds.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/imsi-vs-imei-what-your-phone-broadcasts-and-who-can-read-it/
