1. CHECK POINT GATEWAYS FALL TO PHP2SHELL AND A GAIA OS BUG
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch, apt]
   Latest developments: Check Point shipped emergency hotfixes
   on November 13, 2026 for a high-severity flaw attackers have
   exploited since late October to take root and hold
   persistence, and the company detailed a campaign that started
   October 30, 2026 against Israeli organizations: an
   unidentified actor entered unpatched Security Gateways
   through the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities, escalated to root with
   CVE-2026-123039, a previously unreported local privilege
   escalation in Gaia OS, installed a backdoor named TSGA, and
   deleted files on connected VMware ESXi hosts, with a wider
   wave following.
   Check Point Security Gateways sit at the perimeter of
   enterprise networks and route all traffic through Gaia OS.
   Administrators should install the hotfixes immediately, hunt
   for TSGA, and check ESXi datastores for deleted files.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/check-point-patches-zero-day-that-lets-hackers-get-root-access/

2. OPERATION TORNADO ARRESTS 354 DARK WEB TRADERS
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [law enforcement, malware,
   credential theft]
   Latest developments: Europol and police in 14 countries
   arrested 354 dark web vendors and buyers in Operation
   TorNado, which ran from May 2025 to October 2026 and worked
   backward from five marketplaces already taken
   offline—Archetyp, Bohemia, Nemesis, Kingdom, and Tor2Door—as
   well as forums and single-vendor shops; separately,
   investigators put this week's Operation Endgame round at 235
   servers and 257 domains seized behind Gootloader, CastleRAT,
   and StealC, with 6.4 million stolen logins recovered from
   more than 40,000 victims, according to Germany's Federal
   Criminal Police Office.
   Both operations show that a marketplace or botnet takedown
   leaves a usable evidence trail against the people who traded
   on it. Organizations should assume credentials harvested by
   StealC and Gootloader circulate now and force resets on
   accounts appearing in the recovered sets.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/13/europol-dark-web-arrests-cybercrime/

5. CLICKFIX LURES WEAR ZILLOW, TEAMS, AND AI BRANDS
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [phishing, malware, social
   engineering]
   Latest developments: EfficientIP Research Labs traced a
   ClickFix campaign that dressed its pages as Zillow apartment
   reviews, copied a PowerShell command to the visitor's
   clipboard behind a routine-looking verification check, and
   installed NetSupport RAT on Windows machines with its window
   hidden and a restart at every login; the browser security
   company Island counted 186 lookalike domains impersonating AI
   tools, a pattern it calls TypoPrompting, that serve the same
   verification lures along with browser-hijacking malware and
   search-hijacking extensions; and Huntress documented
   attackers who used a fake Microsoft Teams meeting scheduler
   to talk an employee at a construction company into typing a
   device code into Microsoft's genuine sign-in page, which
   handed them a valid session token before the Huntress
   security operations center caught it.
   All three campaigns ask the victim to perform one
   normal-looking step—paste a command, enter a code—and that
   step delivers remote access or a session token. Blocking
   clipboard-to-PowerShell execution, disabling the device code
   sign-in flow where business needs allow, and filtering
   lookalike AI domains cut off the common path.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/13/fake-zillow-clickfix-netsupport-rat/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/186-fake-ai-domains-redirect-users-to.html
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/13/microsoft-teams-device-code-phishing/

6. SILENTAIR INTERCEPTS SMS CODES INSIDE ASIAN CARRIERS
   Nation-State Activity · [apt, telecom, espionage]
   Latest developments: Enea, the Stockholm telecommunications
   and cybersecurity software company, named a new espionage
   group SilentAir this week, reporting that it operates hacked
   equipment inside Asian mobile operators to intercept the
   calls and text messages of selected people, most of them in
   Taiwan, and that its attack sources trace to at least six
   Asian countries; Enea assesses with high confidence that a
   state runs it.
   SilentAir reads traffic from inside carrier networks, which
   hands it the one-time codes that SMS two-factor
   authentication delivers. Any organization protecting accounts
   with text-message codes should move high-value users to
   hardware keys or app-based authentication.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/silentair-hijacks-asian-telecom.html
