1. CISA FLAGS EXPLOITED N-ABLE MAILASSURE FLAW
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, breach, zero-day]
   CISA added N-able MailAssure flaw CVE-2026-134624, a
   server-side request forgery rated 9.3, to its Known Exploited
   Vulnerabilities catalog on Monday, citing active
   exploitation.
   Why it matters: The bug sits in the email filtering cloud
   N-able runs for managed service providers, handing attackers
   a route to internal systems behind that service.
   - Zoom in: N-able found the flaw while investigating the
     breach of its Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud. A
     user with high-level API access could open connections to
     chosen internal systems.
   - What to do: Apply the N-able patch and audit high-level API
     accounts. The company restored access to the affected
     services and continues to monitor those environments.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-n-able-mailassure-flaw.html
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/n-able-discloses-critical-mail-assure.html
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/n-able-patches-critical-flaw-disclosed-after-mail-assure-cyberattack/

2. NORWAY NAMES EQUINOR IN AZURE TOKEN HUNT
   Critical Infrastructure Security · [cloud, credentials]
   Norway's National Security Authority said an unknown actor
   hunted access tokens, secrets, and named values in Azure API
   Management, hitting energy firms Equinor and Skagerak Energi.
   Why it matters: Stolen gateway credentials reach the backend
   systems an API fronts, turning one cloud flaw into access
   across Norwegian energy operations and other sectors.
   - What to do: Microsoft closed the flaw in Azure API
     Management Classic tiers. Rotate every secret, named value,
     and subscription key stored in the service.
   - Yes, but: The National Security Authority says it remains
     uncertain how the actor exploited the vulnerability.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/microsoft-fixes-azure-api-management.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-azure-flaw-exploited-to-steal-customers-api-credentials/
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/azure-flaw-exploited-to-target-secrets-at-norwegian-energy-firms/

3. CAIRN AGENTS REACH ROOT IN 11 MINUTES
   AI Security · [ai, zero-day, apt]
   Dark Reading reported that a China-nexus actor's Cairn agents
   moved from finding the MONAI Label zero-day to root access in
   11 minutes at East Asian universities.
   Why it matters: Agentic tooling collapses the gap between
   flaw discovery and full compromise to minutes, far inside
   most patch and detection cycles.
   - What to do: CISA added the MONAI Label flaw to its Known
     Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Monday. Patch the medical
     imaging platform and block unauthenticated file writes
     outside its storage folder.
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/ai-agents-flaw-discovery-root-access-11-minutes
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-n-able-mailassure-flaw.html

4. OPENAI AND CISCO SHIP SHIRUDO SHIELD
   AI Security · [ai, patch]
   OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, an open-source Linux
   runtime that lets AI agents write dynamic eBPF controls
   blocking exploit behavior with no restart.
   Why it matters: Testing and deploying a Linux patch takes
   days or weeks, and the vulnerable service stays exposed the
   whole time.
   - What to do: Teams running exposed Linux services can deploy
     a shield that blocks the specific behavior a vulnerability
     depends on while they test the vendor fix.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openai-and-cisco-unveil-ai-assisted.html
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/24/cisco-openai-shirudo/

5. UNSECURED DATABASES EXPOSE 3.5 BILLION RECORDS
   Data Breaches · [breach, credentials]
   Wired reported that a pair of unsecured databases exposed 3.5
   billion usernames, passwords, and documents tied to hundreds
   of millions of people.
   Why it matters: A corpus that size fuels credential stuffing
   and account takeover, and the mix of logins and documents
   lets an attacker own a victim's digital life.
   - What to do: Force resets on reused corporate passwords and
     enforce multi-factor authentication. Watch customer login
     endpoints for credential stuffing spikes.
   - Wired Security: https://www.wired.com/story/3-billion-usernames-passwords-and-documents-leaked-online/
