1. PHP2SHELL EXPLOITATION BEGAN BEFORE DISCLOSURE
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch, exploit]
   Latest developments: Amazon's threat intelligence team said
   its MadPot honeypot service logged an exploit attempt from a
   commercial anonymization network on October 17, 2026, four
   days before the PHP Foundation disclosed CVE-2026-109618 and
   CVE-2026-109619, and broad scanning followed within hours of
   the patches; Searchlight Cyber released php2shell-scanner, an
   open-source tool that tests a host for both bugs, and
   researchers confirm exploitation against WordPress, Drupal,
   Joomla, phpMyAdmin, Nextcloud, and Magento installations;
   Palo Alto Networks confirmed that every version of PAN-OS and
   Panorama carries the flaws and has shipped no fixed release,
   Fortinet is checking 12 products and Cisco 31 products and
   cloud services; Bruce Schneier put the highest risk on Apache
   with mod_php, where researchers demonstrated a working
   exploit; and 404 Media reported security practitioners
   calling the month Black October as the PHP bugs landed atop a
   run of supply chain compromises.
   PHP2Shell is a pair of critical flaws in the PHP runtime that
   run an unauthenticated attacker's code on any server
   answering web requests, regardless of the application on top.
   CISA tells organizations to move to PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27,
   8.3.36, or 8.2.35 immediately; customers of appliances that
   embed PHP, among them PAN-OS and Panorama, must wait on
   vendor builds and restrict management interfaces meanwhile.

2. EMUDHRA QUITS THE TLS BUSINESS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [cryptography, pki, supply
   chain]
   Latest developments: The board of eMudhra, the Indian
   certificate authority that advertises more than 60 million
   certificates issued, voted on Thursday, October 22, 2026 to
   withdraw from the global SSL/TLS certificate business, five
   days after a researcher recovered the private keys of eight
   of its intermediate certificate authority certificates; Scott
   Helme published the detail that sank the company, reporting
   that one private key, which should hold a random 384-bit
   number, held the number four, a botched key-signing ceremony
   that let anyone mint trusted web credentials for any site;
   eMudhra says partners will issue replacement certificates to
   affected customers at no charge, and Unit 42 published a
   containment guide for organizations that maintain their own
   trust decisions.
   A certificate authority's intermediate private keys let the
   holder forge TLS certificates for any domain and intercept
   encrypted traffic. Google, Mozilla, Apple, and the Go project
   already block the eight keys; administrators running their
   own trust stores or pinning should remove the eMudhra
   intermediates and plan certificate replacement.
   - Ars Technica Security: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/disgraced-emudhra-drops-out-of-tls-business-following-certificate-debacle/

6. CLAUDE AUDITS OPEN SOURCE AS AGENT PERMISSIONS SPREAD
   AI Security · [ai, supply chain, research]
   Latest developments: Anthropic's Frontier Red Team reported
   on October 22, 2026 that it used Claude to examine 25
   open-source projects and found 493 problems involving
   unnecessary exposure, conflicting settings, and code that
   runs before security checks, among them 94 cases where a
   project drops security controls with no warning; a survey of
   1,100 information technology and security decision-makers
   found 70% now let AI make decisions and take actions that
   needed a person's sign-off a year ago, with three in four
   organizations hitting at least one AI-related security
   problem or warning sign in the past year; CISA and the
   Canadian Centre for Cyber Security published joint guidance
   on managing AI token consumption, warning that unmanaged
   usage drives unpredictable cost and can cut access to
   critical services; and researchers at Imperial College London
   tampered with speech-to-text models so that one chosen
   speaker's words transcribed as text the attacker picked, in
   one test a request to send $5,000 that AI agents reading the
   transcript executed in a sandboxed banking environment.
   The findings share a root: organizations hand agents
   authority faster than they inspect the code and models
   underneath. Teams running agents should cut exposed
   functionality in the software agents touch, require approval
   for financial actions, and treat fine-tuning access to a
   speech model as a privileged pipeline.

10. APT41 BOOTKIT AND SANDWORM WIPER
   Nation-State Activity · [apt, malware, wiper]
   Latest developments: Kaspersky's Global Research and Analysis
   Team disclosed BlackX on October 22, 2026, a UEFI bootkit
   built along the lines of BlackLotus that the Chinese group
   APT41 revived against diplomatic organizations in Central
   Asia, exploiting the Baton Drop flaw CVE-2022-21894 in the
   Windows boot manager to run before the operating system
   loads; separately, researchers said on October 21, 2026 that
   the Russian state-linked group Sandworm worked with a second
   threat actor last year to destroy data at a Ukrainian grain
   logistics company.

   reinstall, which puts boot-chain integrity and firmware
   monitoring at the center of any response. Diplomatic missions
   in Central Asia and Ukrainian agricultural logistics firms
   face the two campaigns; both call for verified offline
   backups and checks that Secure Boot revocations for
   CVE-2022-21894 sit in place.
