1. CAIRN AGENTS EXPLOIT MONAI LABEL ZERO-DAY
   Nation-State Activity · [zero-day, ai, apt]
   Amazon Threat Intelligence pinned the agent-discovered flaw
   to MONAI Label 0.8.5 and earlier in default setups, where an
   attacker with no account writes files outside the storage
   folder.
   Why it matters: Every internet-exposed MONAI Label server in
   that configuration offers suspected China-linked operators
   the same unauthenticated path to root that took a university
   server.
   - What to do: Locate MONAI Label deployments, move off 0.8.5
     and earlier, and pull the servers off the public internet.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/amazon-china-linked-hackers-ai-agents-exploited-medical-software-zero-day/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-ai-agents-found-zero-day-in.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-use-autonomous-ai-tool-cairn-to-find-zero-day-take-over-server/

2. FBI INVESTIGATES HACK OF NCIC DATABASE
   Data Breaches · [breach, government]
   The FBI opened an investigation into an apparent hack of the
   National Crime Information Center, the criminal justice
   database it maintains, an Associated Press source said.
   Why it matters: NCIC moves records between local, state, and
   federal law enforcement agencies, so stolen or altered
   entries touch active cases across the country.
   - The big picture: This is the second reported intrusion into
     FBI systems in two months.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/fbi-investigating-apparent-hack-of-its-key-national-criminal-justice-database-ap-source-says/
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fbi-reportedly-hacked-again-this-time-targeting-nationwide-police-database/

3. OPENROUTER DROPS THREE AI INFERENCE PROVIDERS
   AI Security · [ai, breach, supply-chain]
   OpenRouter cut three AI inference providers from its routing
   pool after Dragos found attackers had stolen customer
   requests from inside them for months.
   Why it matters: Prompts carry source code, credentials, and
   customer records, so a breached inference provider leaks the
   data enterprises assumed disappeared on arrival.
   - Between the lines: Some stolen requests carried the Zero
     Data Retention flag, the setting that promises a prompt is
     never stored.
   - By the numbers: Researchers planted 2 million trackable
     canary prompts into the AI supply chain and watched secret
     user data leak.
   - What to do: Inventory which downstream providers your AI
     gateway routes prompts to. Treat retention promises as
     unverified until the provider proves detection works.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openrouter-drops-3-providers-after.html
   - Ars Technica Security: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/

4. SIGNAL BLOCKS AI AGENTS FROM SCREENSHOTTING CHATS
   AI Security · [ai, privacy, mobile]
   Signal will block screenshots on iOS 27 and stop assistants
   including Apple's Siri and Google's Gemini from capturing
   conversations on supported Android devices.
   Why it matters: On-device agents read whatever the screen
   displays, so end-to-end encryption stops protecting a message
   the moment it renders.
   - Zoom in: The restriction covers agentic systems built into
     iOS 27 and Android versions where Signal can detect an
     assistant trying to read the app.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/signal-blocks-ai-agents-from-reading.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/signal-to-block-siri-gemini-ai-from-capturing-chats-on-ios-android/

5. HACKERS EXPLOIT GL.INET CLOUD TO PLANT BACKDOORS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [iot, malware, exploit]
   GL.iNet confirmed that hackers exploited a flaw in its cloud
   remote management services to install custom malware on
   customer routers and KVM devices.
   Why it matters: A compromised router hands attackers a
   persistent foothold inside home and small-business networks
   and a view of the traffic passing through.
   - Zoom in: Affected users report backdoors and reverse shells
     that give remote access to infected devices.
   - What to do: Turn off GL.iNet cloud remote management.
     Inspect routers and KVM devices for unexpected listeners
     and outbound connections.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-glinet-cloud-flaw-to-infect-routers-kvm-devices/

6. CLEVERBRIDGE BREACH EXPOSES CARD NUMBERS AND CVVS
   Data Breaches · [breach, payments, ecommerce]
   Cleverbridge warned that hackers breached its systems and
   stole customer payment card information, including credit
   card numbers and CVV security codes.
   Why it matters: Card numbers paired with CVVs enable
   immediate card-not-present fraud, and Cleverbridge runs
   checkout for software vendors worldwide.
   - What to do: Replace cards used on Cleverbridge checkout
     pages and watch those accounts for fraudulent charges.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cleverbridge-warns-hackers-stole-credit-card-numbers-cvvs-in-data-breach/

7. PWN.AI BREAKS GHOSTSCRIPT SANDBOX WITH UPLOADS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [rce, patch, open-source]
   Security firm pwn.ai showed that a crafted file escapes
   Ghostscript's security sandbox and runs commands on the
   server that processes it.
   Why it matters: Linux servers call Ghostscript to convert
   uploaded PDFs, PostScript files, and images, often without
   the application knowing, so an ordinary upload form becomes
   the entry point.
   - What to do: Install the Ghostscript fix. Map which
     applications hand user-supplied documents to Ghostscript
     for conversion.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/researchers-demonstrate-ghostscript.html

8. COM AFFILIATES STEAL CLOUD DATA BY PHONE
   Data Breaches · [social-engineering, extortion, cloud]
   Unit 42 detailed how Com affiliates phone employees at
   healthcare and finance firms, hijack their accounts, and pull
   data out of cloud services for extortion.
   Why it matters: Voice phishing walks past technical controls
   and reaches cloud stores holding patient and customer records
   within hours of a single successful call.
   - What to do: Harden help desk identity checks before any
     password or multifactor reset. Alert on bulk downloads from
     cloud data stores.
   - Unit 42 (Palo Alto): https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/com-affiliate-vishing-attacks/
