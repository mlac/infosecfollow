1. IRELAND FINES GOOGLE $463 MILLION OVER LOCATION DATA
   Policy and Regulation · [policy, privacy]
   Latest developments: Ireland's Data Protection Commission
   fined Google 403 million euros, or $463 million, on September
   21, 2026 for the way it processed users' location data, and
   ordered the company to bring that processing into compliance

2. FAKE LASTPASS INSTALLERS KILL 145 SECURITY PRODUCTS
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, stealer]
   Latest developments: The Hacker News added the detail that
   makes the Rapuncel campaign work on September 21, 2026:
   Microsoft's own hardware-compatibility program signed the
   kernel driver that shuts off antivirus and endpoint detection
   before the stealer runs, and the driver scored zero
   detections on VirusTotal when researchers at LastPass and
   Delphos Labs tested it; SecurityWeek reported the same day
   that the crew impersonates at least 40 companies and disables
   145 security products, while Akshay Gaikwad and Aaron
   Beardslee of Securonix Threat Research dissected TASK#STOMP,
   a PowerShell backdoor that hunts business documents across a
   victim's drives, watches the file system for each new or
   edited file, and lifts saved Wi-Fi passwords, clipboard text,
   and screenshots.
   Rapuncel reaches victims through search-optimized GitHub
   repositories and download pages dressed as well-known
   software, and its signed driver shuts down endpoint agents
   before they report anything. Block unsigned and
   known-vulnerable kernel drivers, restrict who may install
   drivers, and pull software only from vendor domains.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/fake-lastpass-installers-push-kernel-level-edr-killer-rapuncel-stealer/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/fake-lastpass-authenticator-installer.html

5. CHAINSCRIPT AND TERMINALFIX BURY THEIR COMMAND CHANNELS
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, rat]
   Latest developments: Blackpoint's Adversary Pursuit Group
   documented ChainScript on September 21, 2026, a previously
   undocumented remote access trojan that ClickFix-style lures
   deliver under build names including ComponentTask33,
   UpdateDigital, HostShared, and OrchidViolet66 while posing as
   Spotify, Zoom Workplace, and Microsoft Teams, and that reads
   fresh command-server addresses off the Polygon blockchain;
   the SANS Internet Storm Center the same day pulled apart the
   PNG files that carry payloads in the TerminalFix campaign
   Microsoft Security Research detailed, which opens a reverse
   tunnel through a multistage intrusion.
   Both campaigns hide the parts defenders usually catch, one by
   parking its command and control pointers on a public
   blockchain nobody can take down, the other by stuffing code
   inside ordinary image files. Security teams should hunt for
   outbound traffic to blockchain RPC endpoints and inspect PNG
   files that arrive with software installers.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/clickfix-lures-deploy-chainscript-rat.html
   - SANS Internet Storm Center: https://isc.sans.edu/diary/rss/33318

6. META'S MUSE ASSISTANT CARRIES A HIJACKABLE ZERO-DAY
   AI Security · [ai, zero-day]

8. PAYLOAD RANSOMWARE HIJACKS GROUP POLICY
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [ransomware, active-directory]
   Latest developments: Kaspersky's Global Emergency Response
   Team published a technical incident analysis on September 21,
   2026 of PAYLOAD, an encryptionless, binary-less ransomware
   operation that abuses the Active Directory mechanisms for
   managing Group Policy Objects to carry out its attack.
   PAYLOAD leaves no encryptor on disk and instead drives the
   intrusion through the same Group Policy machinery that
   administrators use to configure every domain-joined
   workstation. Defenders should alert on the creation and
   modification of Group Policy Objects, tighten who holds edit
   rights, and keep SYSVOL backups.
   - Securelist (Kaspersky): https://securelist.com/tr/payload-ransomware-via-group-policy/121335/

9. RATHAT TROJAN DRIVES ANDROID PHONES WITH AI
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, ai, mobile]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek described RatHat on
   September 21, 2026, an Android trojan that leans on
   artificial intelligence to navigate and control an infected
   phone in real time, which lets it adapt to whatever screen it
   lands on and sidestep detection.
   RatHat gives its operator live command of a victim's handset,

   hard-coded screen recipes for each banking or wallet app.
   Mobile fleet owners should block sideloaded installs,
   restrict accessibility-service permissions, and watch for
   apps that request them.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/rathat-android-trojan-uses-ai-for-automation/
