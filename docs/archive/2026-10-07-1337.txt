4. FORTIBLEED LOCKS ADMINS OUT OF FORTINET FIREWALLS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [exploit, credentials]
   Latest developments: The FBI and the United States Secret
   Service issued a joint advisory on Tuesday, October 6, 2026
   saying the FortiBleed credential-harvesting campaign against
   internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN
   gateways remains active, cited SOCRadar's verified count of
   86,644 compromised devices in 194 countries, and warned that
   victims lose access to their own Fortinet devices when the
   attacker deletes or alters administrator credentials.
   FortiBleed works through reused and leaked credentials plus
   Fortinet's legacy SHA-256 password storage, which hands
   attackers working logins on gateways that terminate remote
   access for whole organizations. Administrators should rotate
   every account on the appliance, pull credentials that predate
   the current password scheme, restrict management interfaces,
   and check administrator lists for deletions and additions.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/07/fortinet-fortibleed-campaign-fbi-advisory/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/fbi-warns-fortibleed-remains-active.html
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/fortibleed-warning-fbi-secret-service

7. COUNTERFEIT TLS CERTIFICATES OUT OF THREE REGISTRIES
   Identity and Trust Infrastructure · [dns, certificates]
   Latest developments: Google named the three country-code
   domain registries on Tuesday, October 6, 2026: attackers
   compromised the third-party operators of .gh for Ghana, .sl
   for Sierra Leone, and .as for American Samoa, changed the
   authoritative DNS records, and used that control to obtain
   HTTPS certificates for several Google domains and for domains
   belonging to other large organizations.
   Control of a registry's DNS puts every domain under that
   ending at risk, because certificate authorities validate
   ownership through DNS and will issue to whoever answers.
   Organizations holding domains under .gh, .sl, or .as should
   check Certificate Transparency logs for certificates they
   never requested and revoke them.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/07/google-unauthorized-https-certificates-cctld-hijacks/
