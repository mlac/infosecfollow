1. LAUNDRY BEAR ZIMBRA ESPIONAGE DRAWS JOINT INTERNATIONAL
   ADVISORY
   Nation-State Activity · [apt, zero-day, espionage]
   Latest developments: The NSA, CISA, and partner agencies
   published joint advisory AA26-204A on July 23, 2026, formally
   documenting the Russian state-supported group Laundry Bear
   compromising Western government and commercial organizations
   running Zimbra Collaboration Suite since at least July 2025,
   while Palo Alto's Unit 42 detailed the JavaScript injection
   and Dark Reading described the half-click emails that require
   a victim only to open or preview a message.
   Laundry Bear, also tracked as Void Blizzard, exploits a
   now-patched Zimbra webmail flaw to lift the last 90 days of
   mail, the full organizational email directory, browser-saved
   passwords, and two-factor recovery codes from targets across
   the US and Ukraine. Zimbra administrators should apply the
   patch and hunt for the injected JavaScript.
   - CISA Advisories: https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-204a
   - Unit 42 (Palo Alto): https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/russian-webmail-espionage/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/russian-espionage-group-exploited.html
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/russian-hackers-zimbra-zero-day-us-ukraine-targets

3. CHAOS RANSOMWARE ROUTES C2 THROUGH THE VICTIM'S BROWSER
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [ransomware, malware]
   Latest developments: Cisco Talos detailed msaRAT, a Rust
   implant it attributes to the Chaos ransomware group that
   never opens its own outbound connection but instead launches
   Chrome or Edge in headless mode, drives them through the
   Chrome DevTools Protocol, and tunnels command-and-control
   over a WebRTC channel that hides the attacker's IP from the
   victim.
   Found on a compromised Windows machine ahead of the
   encryptor, msaRAT enables arbitrary command execution while
   its traffic blends into ordinary browser activity. Defenders
   should watch for unexpected headless browser processes and
   DevTools debugging activity.
   - Cisco Talos: https://blog.talosintelligence.com/chaos-msarat-living-off-the-browser-to-build-covert-c2-channel/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/chaos-ransomware-uses-msarat-to-route.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-msarat-malware-uses-chrome-edge-browsers-to-route-c2-traffic/

   AI Security · [ai, vulnerability, malware]
   Latest developments: OpenAI fixed AgentForger, a ChatGPT
   Agent flaw that let an attacker create, insert, and remotely
   control an invisible autonomous AI agent inside a victim
   organization, while Accomplish AI disclosed a sandbox escape

   Organizations deploying agentic AI should enforce
   least-privilege access, isolate agent execution, and monitor
   for unauthorized agent creation.
