4. SECURE BOOT BYPASSABLE FOR 13 YEARS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [vulnerability, firmware,
   patch]
   Latest developments: ESET researchers reported that
   Microsoft's Secure Boot, an industry-wide standard meant to
   block firmware infections on Windows and Linux devices, has
   been trivial to bypass for 13 of its 14 years after they
   identified 11 firmware images, at least one dating to 2013,
   that Microsoft kept signing despite knowing they were
   defective.
   Secure Boot verifies firmware integrity at startup to stop
   bootkits, and the long-lived signing failure let attackers
   slip past that protection on affected hardware.
   Administrators should track Microsoft's revocation updates
   and confirm their firmware images fall outside the flagged,
   defective set.
   - Schneier on Security: https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/07/long-lived-vulnerability-in-microsoft-secure-boot.html
