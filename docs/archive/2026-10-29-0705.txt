1. CISCO VPN ZERO-DAY LANDS IN A PATCH WAVE
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek reported on October 29,
   2026 that attackers exploit a flaw in the Remote Access SSL
   VPN service of Cisco ASA and FTD firewalls, forcing the
   devices to reload without authenticating, the second Cisco
   firewall VPN zero-day since August 2026; Adobe closed eight
   critical Campaign Classic flaws led by CVE-2026-104351 at
   9.9, Google shipped Chrome 156 with 52 fixes, Mozilla shipped
   Firefox 159 with 167, and FoxIO disclosed three TLS bugs in
   the GoAhead web server that runs on hundreds of millions of
   devices.
   The Cisco bug gives an unauthenticated attacker a reliable
   way to knock ASA and FTD firewalls offline, and the GoAhead
   flaws let revoked certificates pass validation and expose
   some encrypted sessions to eavesdroppers. Prioritize the
   Cisco VPN fix and the Adobe Campaign Classic updates, then
   roll the browser releases.

2. KEYDRIFT STEALS CISCO SD-WAN ENCRYPTION KEYS
   Nation-State Activity · [apt, zero-day, patch]
   Latest developments: Cisco Talos disclosed UAT-11473, an
   advanced group that exploited CVE-2026-20127, the
   authentication bypass in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller
   instances Cisco fixed in February 2026, to deploy custom
   malware called KeyDrift, and Talos tied the attack
   infrastructure to Volt Typhoon's Silent Train covert access
   network that Lumen Technologies Black Lotus Labs described
   this week.
   KeyDrift runs inside the controllers that govern an
   organization's SD-WAN fabric and copies the keys that encrypt
   traffic between sites, storing them for later decryption.
   Operators should apply the February fix for CVE-2026-20127,
   rotate SD-WAN keys, and hunt for the indicators Talos
   published.
   - Cisco Talos: https://blog.talosintelligence.com/uat-11473-keydrift/
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-sd-wan-zero-day-exploitation-tied-to-volt-typhoons-silent-train-network/

3. WARP PANDA BLINDS VMWARE ESX LOGS
   Nation-State Activity · [apt, zero-day, patch]
   Latest developments: CrowdStrike attributed CVE-2026-41709 to
   Warp Panda, a China-nexus group that used the low-severity
   Broadcom VMware ESX logging flaw as a zero-day against
   targets in the United States legal sector, and CISA added the
   bug to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October
   28, 2026 alongside CVE-2026-46300, a missing variable
   initialization in the Linux kernel.
   The flaw lets an attacker who already holds administrative
   privileges on an ESX host carry out operations that leave no
   log entry, stripping investigators of the audit trail.
   Broadcom customers should patch and reconcile hypervisor
   activity against logs stored off the host.

5. AI TOOLS TURN INTO ATTACKER INFRASTRUCTURE
   AI Security · [ai, malware]
   Latest developments: Cisco Talos reported on October 29, 2026
   that it found NIK C2, a command-and-control framework with AI
   built into it, operating in the wild; Dark Reading described
   a relay service that supplies OpenAI Codex and Claude Code to
   mainland China and other regions where neither company sells,
   with the tools' sandbox protections switched off; and a
   separate actor scanned the internet for exposed credentials
   from behind bots posing as crawlers from OpenAI, Anthropic,
   and Meta.
   Attackers now build AI into their own command infrastructure,
   strip safety controls out of commercial coding agents for
   resale, and borrow the reputation of AI company crawlers to
   scan for secrets unchallenged. Defenders should verify
   crawler identities instead of trusting user-agent strings and
   watch for coding agents running outside sanctioned channels.
