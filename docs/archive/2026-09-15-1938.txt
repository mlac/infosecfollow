1. CISCO SECURE EMAIL GATEWAY ZERO-DAY HANDS ATTACKERS ROOT
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch, kev]
   Latest developments: Cisco released the patch and confirmed
   active exploitation on September 15, 2026, detailing
   CVE-2026-76461 as a 9.8-rated failure to validate email
   parsing in AsyncOS versions 15.5, 16.0, and 16.5 that lets an
   unauthenticated remote attacker run arbitrary commands as
   root, published indicators of compromise for administrators
   to hunt with, and sharpened the SQL injection entry CISA
   posted to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog a day
   earlier.
   Cisco Secure Email Gateway appliances handle inbound mail for
   large enterprises, and a crafted message reaches the
   underlying operating system with full privileges.
   Administrators should install the AsyncOS fix immediately and
   audit the appliances for unfamiliar accounts and outbound
   connections.

2. POISONED PLUGINS BACKDOOR WORDPRESS AND CPANEL HOSTS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [supply-chain, wordpress,
   exploit]
   Latest developments: A threat actor compromised the website
   of the developer who maintains Admin Menu Editor Pro and
   pushed tampered updates to more than 200 customers, planting
   a hidden administrator account on 1,500 WordPress sites;
   hackers separately exploit a critical flaw in the WooCommerce
   Wholesale Lead Capture premium plugin to upload a PHP
   backdoor; Acronis disclosed a high-severity Linux privilege
   escalation bug in its backup plugin for cPanel, WebHost
   Manager, and Plesk that attackers may already exploit; and
   cPanel warned on September 14, 2026 that a LiteSpeed Web
   Server Enterprise flaw lets the holder of one low-privilege
   hosting account take root on a shared server and reach every
   other site on the machine.
   Shared hosting stacks pack thousands of customer sites onto
   single machines, and the plugins and control panels that
   manage them update automatically from vendor servers. Site
   owners should audit WordPress installs for administrator
   accounts nobody created, update the named plugins, and press
   hosting providers for their LiteSpeed and cPanel patch
   status.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malcious-admin-menu-editor-pro-plugin-backdoors-1-500-wordpress-sites/
