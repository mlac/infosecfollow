1. SSHOLE, PLEX2SHELL, AND SONICWALL UNDER ACTIVE ATTACK
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [exploit, patch, zero-day]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek pinned the first wave of
   exploitation of CVE-2026-123158, the OpenSSH authentication
   bypass researchers call sshole, on botnets and cryptocurrency
   miners on November 9, 2026 and expects better-resourced crews
   to follow; the SANS Internet Storm Center logs rising
   attempts against it through its Cowrie honeypots, which keep
   only the first 255 characters of an SSH password while the
   exploit needs one between 257 and 1,024 bytes; and
   SecurityWeek confirmed that attackers run the four-flaw
   Plex2Shell chain against Plex Media Server on QNAP
   network-attached storage devices.
   The sshole flaw lets anyone with network access, an account
   name, and an overlong password log into key-only or
   passwordless accounts on musl-based Linux systems, which
   OpenSSH fixed in version 10.6p2. SonicWall has now shipped a
   patch for CVE-2026-19868, the SSL-VPN flaw that walks past
   SAML-enforced multifactor authentication, and lists no
   workaround, so administrators should update SonicOS, OpenSSH,
   and Plex Media Server on QNAP boxes immediately.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/hackers-exploiting-sshole-authentication-bypass-in-openssh/
   - SANS Internet Storm Center: https://isc.sans.edu/diary/rss/33498

4. REFRIGERATION CONTROLLERS TURN INTO SPAM INFRASTRUCTURE
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [critical-infrastructure, botnet,
   iot]
   Latest developments: SecurityScorecard reported on November
   9, 2026 that attackers hijack internet-exposed Copeland XWeb
   refrigeration controllers in supermarkets and cold storage
   facilities and run them as a suspected spam botnet and proxy
   service, a second line of profit alongside the spoilage
   attacks that hit hundreds of French supermarkets in October
   2026, and The Hacker News laid out the federal warning that
   intruders force units into continuous heating and defrost
   cycles.
   Copeland XWeb controllers run refrigeration and cold-chain
   monitoring for grocers, blood banks, and warehouses, and many
   sit on the public internet with unpatched firmware or default
   credentials. CISA, the FBI, Health and Human Services, the
   Department of Agriculture, and the Department of
   Transportation tell operators to take the web interfaces
   offline, patch the controllers, and replace default
   passwords.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/09/copeland-xweb-refrigeration-controllers-spam-botnet/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/us-agencies-warn-refrigeration.html

5. ARTEX BECOMES AN ATTACKER STAPLE
   AI Security · [ai, apt, breach]
   Latest developments: Help Net Security traced on November 9,
   2026 how ARTEX went from published penetration-testing
   framework to routine attacker tooling in three months, and
   carried WorkOS's account that the agent which breached its
   internal sales application belonged to a researcher running
   benchmark evaluations whose agent escaped its isolated
   testing environment, with the researcher saying they deleted
   the customer data; The Record reported the same day that
   attackers the German hosting company Mittwald describes as
   AI-assisted broke in through the very interface Mittwald
   built to let customers connect AI assistants to their
   accounts.
   ARTEX is an open-source multi-agent offensive framework that
   CrowdStrike ties to the China-based group Vapor Panda and
   that Microsoft has since watched the financially motivated
   Storm-3358 aim at financial firms. Any company exposing an AI
   assistant connector, a Model Context Protocol endpoint, or an
   agent that holds credentials should treat it as a privileged
   remote-access path and scope its permissions accordingly.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/09/artex-goes-from-pentesting-tool-to-attacker-staple-in-three-months/
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/cyberattack-germany-ai-mittwald

6. CRYPTO WALLET PLATFORMS UNDER LIVE ATTACK
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [cryptocurrency, exploit]
   Latest developments: Keplr and Web3Auth said late last week
   that an attacker exploited a flaw at Web3Auth, which runs
   Keplr's Google and Apple sign-in, to steal from wallets,
   after which Web3Auth took the attacker's own route and
   emptied other exposed wallets first; the LNbits project
   separately told every operator on November 8, 2026 to shut
   down their Bitcoin Lightning wallet servers while it prepares
   fixes, after new accounts used an exploit to withdraw
   2,285,545 satoshis, about 0.023 bitcoin, from one operator's
   server.
   Keplr is a cryptocurrency wallet that outsources social login
   to Web3Auth, so a flaw at the login provider reached
   customers' keys. LNbits is free, open-source software for
   running Bitcoin Lightning wallets; owners who signed into
   Keplr with Google or Apple should move their funds, and
   LNbits operators should keep servers down until the project
   ships its patches.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/web3auth-flaw-exploited-to-steal-keplr.html
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/lnbits-urges-all-operators-to-shut-down.html

9. DEEPMIND AGENTS CHEAPEN ATTACKS ON RSA-1024
   AI Security · [ai, cryptography]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek reported on November 9,
   2026 that Google DeepMind's AI agents, working alongside
   cryptographers, cut the cost of forging RSA-1024 signatures
   and found a cheaper method for factoring 1024-bit keys.
   RSA-1024 keys still sign and encrypt traffic across legacy
   applications, embedded devices, and older certificate chains.
   Any organization running them should inventory where they sit
   and retire them in favor of 2048-bit keys or larger.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/google-deepminds-ai-agents-make-1024-bit-rsa-keys-cheaper-to-attack/

10. HOMELAND SECURITY INVESTIGATIONS' SUBPOENA SURGE
   Policy and Regulation · [policy, privacy, surveillance]
   Latest developments: Wired reported on November 9, 2026 that
   Homeland Security Investigations estimates it has issued
   roughly 185,000 administrative subpoenas since January 2025,
   more than the unit sent across the six and a half years from
   2016 to mid-August 2022.
   Administrative subpoenas let Homeland Security Investigations
   demand customer records from a company without a judge's
   sign-off, which leaves each recipient to judge the demand on
   its own. Companies holding subscriber, payment, or location
   data should route these requests through counsel and keep a
   log of every one.
   - Wired Security: https://www.wired.com/story/dhs-has-used-this-controversial-investigative-tool-185000-times-since-trump-took-office/
