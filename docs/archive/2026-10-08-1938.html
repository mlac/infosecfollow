Nation-State Activity · [apt, takedown, espionage]

Latest developments: The FBI seized seven domains on October 8, 2026 that the Chinese state-sponsored group Flax Typhoon used to operate two tools, the vulnerability scanner MicroScan and FishHub, a portal that handed third parties access to stolen email, and CISA, joined by agencies in six other countries, published advisory AA26-281A naming Beijing-based Integrity Technology Group as the company enabling the campaign and listing victims across Southeast Asia: government organizations, law enforcement agencies, healthcare systems, and religious institutions.

2. Shai-Hulud Rides a Compromised npm SDK

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [supply chain, npm, credential theft]

Latest developments: Socket reported on October 8, 2026 that version 0.5.144 of the npm package tensorlake, the TypeScript development kit for Tensorlake sandboxes and cloud services, carries obfuscated malware from the ChainDrop and Shai-Hulud supply chain campaign that harvests credentials, exfiltrates secrets, sets up persistence, and runs code the operator supplies; CloudSEK and Checkmarx separately named a campaign MALFEX, where one author published 12 npm packages since August 2023 and eight of them delivered the Overlord remote access trojan and information stealers across 40,767 downloads; researchers counted 16 malicious Firefox extensions posing as Rabby and OKX wallet portals that intercept recovery phrases and private keys during wallet imports; and the FakeGit campaign reactivated this month with more than 17,610 counterfeit GitHub repositories that push the SmartLoader loader and then the StealC information stealer.

read more

Developers who installed tensorlake 0.5.144 or any of the MALFEX packages should treat every credential on the build host as stolen, rotate tokens, and audit repositories for new persistence. Check the owner and commit history of any unfamiliar GitHub account before running its code or binaries.
