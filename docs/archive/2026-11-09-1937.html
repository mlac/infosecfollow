1. Refrigeration Controllers Turn Into Spam Infrastructure

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [critical-infrastructure, botnet, iot]

Latest developments: French authorities blamed Iran on November 9, 2026 for the October 2026 refrigeration attacks, naming Shahid Kaveh, a cyber unit of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps that investigators previously tied to attacks on industrial controllers at United States water utilities, and counting food losses plus at least 15 fires across more than 700 supermarkets and other retail locations; SecurityScorecard reported the same day that attackers also hijack internet-exposed Copeland XWeb controllers in supermarkets and cold storage facilities and run them as a suspected spam botnet and proxy service.

read more

Copeland XWeb controllers run refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring for grocers, blood banks, and warehouses, and many sit on the public internet with unpatched firmware or default credentials. CISA, the FBI, Health and Human Services, the Department of Agriculture, and the Department of Transportation tell operators to take the web interfaces offline, patch the controllers, and replace default passwords.

2. SSHole, Plex2Shell, and SonicWall Under Active Attack

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [exploit, patch, zero-day]

Latest developments: CISA added four actively exploited flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 9, 2026, among them CVE-2026-123158, the OpenSSH bypass researchers call sshole, alongside CVE-2026-102895 in GPUStack, CVE-2026-116779 in SignalWire FreeSwitch, and CVE-2026-123218 in IBM Aspera Faspex; BleepingComputer and Ars Technica reported that attackers exploiting sshole now execute commands, steal credentials, and hold persistent access on Linux systems, with SecurityWeek pinning the first wave on botnets and cryptocurrency miners; SonicWall shipped a fix for CVE-2026-19868 and listed no workaround; and SecurityWeek confirmed attackers running the four-flaw Plex2Shell chain against Plex Media Server on QNAP network-attached storage devices.

read more

The sshole flaw lets anyone with network access, an account name, and an overlong password log into key-only or passwordless accounts on musl-based Linux systems, which OpenSSH fixed in version 10.6p2. CVE-2026-19868 walks past SAML-enforced multifactor authentication on SonicOS SSL-VPN, so administrators should update SonicOS, OpenSSH, and Plex Media Server on QNAP boxes immediately.

7. Crypto Wallet Platforms Under Live Attack

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [cryptocurrency, exploit]

Latest developments: The Hacker News reported on November 9, 2026 that attackers drove Ethersphere's own release automation to publish malicious bee-js 13.3.0 and Swarm Desktop 0.56.2, which download code that steals the wallet key a Swarm node keeps along with the password that unlocks it and installs a backdoor; Keplr and Web3Auth said late last week that an attacker exploited a flaw at Web3Auth, which runs Keplr's Google and Apple sign-in, to steal from wallets, after which Web3Auth took the attacker's own route and emptied other exposed wallets first; and the LNbits project told every operator on November 8, 2026 to shut down their Bitcoin Lightning wallet servers while it prepares fixes, after new accounts used an exploit to withdraw 2,285,545 satoshis, about 0.023 bitcoin, from one operator's server.

read more

Owners who signed into Keplr with Google or Apple should move their funds, LNbits operators should keep servers down until the project ships its patches, and anyone running the poisoned bee-js or Swarm Desktop builds should treat the node's wallet as lost.

read more
