Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, stealer]

Latest developments: The Hacker News added the detail that makes the Rapuncel campaign work on September 21, 2026: Microsoft's own hardware-compatibility program signed the kernel driver that shuts off antivirus and endpoint detection before the stealer runs, and the driver scored zero detections on VirusTotal when researchers at LastPass and Delphos Labs tested it; SecurityWeek reported the same day that the crew impersonates at least 40 companies and disables 145 security products, while Akshay Gaikwad and Aaron Beardslee of Securonix Threat Research dissected TASK#STOMP, a PowerShell backdoor that hunts business documents across a victim's drives, watches the file system for each new or edited file, and lifts saved Wi-Fi passwords, clipboard text, and screenshots.

read more

5. ChainScript and TerminalFix Bury Their Command Channels

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, rat]

Latest developments: Blackpoint's Adversary Pursuit Group documented ChainScript on September 21, 2026, a previously undocumented remote access trojan that ClickFix-style lures deliver under build names including ComponentTask33, UpdateDigital, HostShared, and OrchidViolet66 while posing as Spotify, Zoom Workplace, and Microsoft Teams, and that reads fresh command-server addresses off the Polygon blockchain; the SANS Internet Storm Center the same day pulled apart the PNG files that carry payloads in the TerminalFix campaign Microsoft Security Research detailed, which opens a reverse tunnel through a multistage intrusion.

read more

Security teams should hunt for outbound traffic to blockchain RPC endpoints and inspect PNG files that arrive with software installers.

8. PAYLOAD Ransomware Hijacks Group Policy

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [ransomware, active-directory]

Latest developments: Kaspersky's Global Emergency Response Team published a technical incident analysis on September 21, 2026 of PAYLOAD, an encryptionless, binary-less ransomware operation that abuses the Active Directory mechanisms for managing Group Policy Objects to carry out its attack.

read more

PAYLOAD leaves no encryptor on disk and instead drives the intrusion through the same Group Policy machinery that administrators use to configure every domain-joined workstation. Defenders should alert on the creation and modification of Group Policy Objects, tighten who holds edit rights, and keep SYSVOL backups.
