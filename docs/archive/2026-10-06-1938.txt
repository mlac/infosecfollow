2. DELL AND ATLASSIAN PATCH PRE-AUTH FLAWS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, vulnerability]
   Latest developments: Dell told customers on October 5, 2026
   to patch CVE-2026-86360, a 9.6-rated path traversal in Dell
   System Update that carries an unauthenticated remote attacker
   to arbitrary code execution with root privileges, and
   Atlassian disclosed CVE-2026-21589 the same day, a 9.3-rated
   flaw that lets an attacker with no login read specific files
   in the web application root directory of eight self-hosted
   Data Center products, Confluence, Jira, and Bitbucket among
   them.
   Dell System Update is the command-line tool enterprise
   administrators use to push driver, BIOS, and firmware updates
   to PowerEdge servers, and every version before 2.3.0.0
   carries the flaw. Upgrade Dell System Update to 2.3.0.0 and
   apply Atlassian's fixes; the Atlassian attacker must know a
   file's exact name and path and cannot list the directory's
   contents.

5. CLICKFIX HIDES ITS PAYLOAD IN THE BROWSER CACHE
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, social engineering]
   Latest developments: Dark Reading reported on October 6, 2026
   that ClickFix crews now also stash payloads in DNS TXT
   records alongside the browser-cache prefetch the Microsoft
   Threat Intelligence team described a day earlier, and CERT-UA
   counted more than 100 compromised websites in Ukraine serving
   fake Cloudflare verification pages that finish by installing
   an infostealer called Lunex.
   ClickFix campaigns talk users into pasting a command into
   Windows Run to fix a fake error or clear a fake CAPTCHA, and
   the cache and DNS tricks remove the remote download that
   defenders watch for. Block script execution from browser
   cache paths, log DNS TXT queries, and train staff that no
   legitimate site asks them to paste a command into Run.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/clickfix-smuggles-payloads-through.html
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/clickfix-campaign-ukraine-lunex-stealer

7. FAKE AI AD PORTALS HARVEST MFA CODES
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [phishing, mfa, ai]
   Latest developments: Researchers disclosed on October 6, 2026
   a human-operated phishing platform that impersonates
   advertising products for OpenAI ChatGPT, Google Gemini,
   Anthropic Claude, Perplexity, Meta Muse, and Manus, offering
   campaign optimization, spend audits, and business-account
   connections, then runs browser-in-browser windows that
   capture the credentials and multifactor codes of the
   advertising managers who sign in.

   the page, so the padlock and address the victim checks belong
   to the attacker's site. Advertising and marketing teams
   should reach ad platforms through bookmarks, move those
   accounts to passkeys or hardware keys, and treat any AI
   advertising tool promising spend audits as a lure.

9. COUNTERFEIT TLS CERTIFICATES OUT OF THREE REGISTRIES
   Identity and Trust Infrastructure · [pki, breach]
   Latest developments: Ars Technica reported on October 6, 2026
   that attackers compromised three domain registries and rode
   that access to obtain unauthorized TLS certificates for
   Google and other large services.
   Certificate authorities issue a TLS certificate after
   checking that the applicant controls the domain, and control
   of a registry lets an attacker pass that check for any name
   the registry holds, then impersonate the site or read traffic
   fronted by it. Site operators should set Certificate
   Authority Authorization records and watch Certificate
   Transparency logs for certificates they never requested.
   - Ars Technica Security: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/hackers-obtain-counterfeit-tls-certificates-for-google-and-other-large-services/
