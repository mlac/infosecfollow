1. PCLOUD SAYS ATTACK EXPOSED USER EMAILS
   Data Breaches · [breach, credentials]
   pCloud said the attack on its pCloud Pass password manager
   exposed a small number of user email addresses and triggered
   login attempts against those accounts.
   Why it matters: Customers trust the Swiss company with every
   credential they own, and users reported unknown devices
   connecting even with two-factor authentication turned on.
   - What they're saying: pCloud said its team promptly
     contained the attack, responding to user reports that
     surfaced on Reddit.
   - What to do: Review connected devices and active sessions in
     pCloud Pass, remove any device you do not recognize, and
     change the account password.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/pcloud-password-manager-hit-by-cyber.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/pcloud-confirms-password-manager-hack-after-suspicious-login-alerts/

2. RESEARCHERS FIND GOD MODE VISA FLAWS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [vulnerability, government,
   critical-infrastructure]
   Researchers disclosed god mode vulnerabilities in the online
   visa and immigration systems of Nigeria, Ethiopia, Zambia,
   Sri Lanka, Fiji, and Ghana.
   Why it matters: The flaws gave attackers a path to disrupt
   traveler entry at six national borders and to reach the
   applicant data behind those portals.
   - The big picture: Border control runs on internet-facing web
     applications. One broken permission check in a visa portal
     reaches the same records an immigration officer sees.
   - What to do: Audit administrative access paths in
     citizen-facing government portals and test for privilege
     escalation that hands ordinary users full system control.
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/vulnerabilities-threats/god-mode-flaws-wreak-border-control-havoc

4. JAPAN EDUCATION MINISTRY DISCLOSES PROCUREMENT BREACH
   Data Breaches · [breach, government]
   Japan's education ministry said hackers breached its
   government procurement system, potentially exposing records
   that hold business information and email addresses.
   Why it matters: Procurement platforms carry supplier and
   contract data that attackers reuse for targeted invoice fraud
   and phishing against government vendors.
   - What to do: Suppliers that bid through the system should
     treat unexpected payment or contract emails as suspect and
     verify requests by phone.
