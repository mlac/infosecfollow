1. DRUPAL PATCH GAP LEAVES SITES EXPLOITABLE
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, exploit, web]
   Drupal disclosed CVE-2026-136716, a highly critical flaw
   already exploited in the wild, because its earlier
   Fail2Install fix left some sites in vulnerable installation
   states.
   Why it matters: Site owners who applied the first patch and
   declared victory still face unauthenticated takeover across
   government, university, and media deployments.
   - What to do: Update Drupal core a second time. Hunt for
     unexpected site reinstalls, new administrator accounts, and
     changes to settings files.
   - Flashback: Attackers have exploited the original bug,
     CVE-2026-132165, since at least November 19, and CISA set a
     November 27 deadline for federal agencies.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/drupal-warns-of-new-highly-critical.html
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/drupal-installer-patch-left-a-gap-attackers-are-already-exploiting/
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/25/drupal-installer-rce-cve-2026-132165-exploited/

2. ATTACKERS EXPLOIT MITEL MICOLLAB ZERO-DAYS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch, exploit]
   Accenture said hackers exploited Mitel MiCollab flaws before
   Mitel published its advisory, likely with AI assistance, in a
   suspected extortion campaign.
   Why it matters: MiCollab servers carry voice, chat, and
   directory data for entire organizations, and the attacks ran
   while no patch existed.
   - By the numbers: CISA added 10 flaws to its Known Exploited
     Vulnerabilities catalog on Wednesday, among them five
     MiCollab bugs plus Juniper, Vates, and Danfoss products.
   - What to do: Patch MiCollab and audit the Audio, Web and
     Video Conferencing component for uploaded files under
     CVE-2026-133275. Pull management interfaces off the
     internet.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/mitel-micollab-zero-days-exploited-in-suspected-ai-assisted-attacks/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-10-vulnerabilities.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisa-warns-of-critical-unpatched-flaw-in-danfoss-refrigeration-controllers/

3. 1PASSWORD SAYS ATTACKER COPIED SOURCE CODE
   Data Breaches · [breach, github]
   1Password said the attacker who hijacked an employee's
   corporate GitHub account copied source code from private
   repositories plus account records, and the investigation
   began November 19, 2026.
   Why it matters: Stolen customer names and emails give
   phishers a vetted list of password manager users to
   impersonate 1Password against.
   - Zoom in: The attacker pushed malicious code into backend
     services before containment. 1Password says customer vaults
     and other secrets stayed inaccessible.
   - What to do: Verify any 1Password support contact through
     the company's own site, and review GitHub account
     protections for developers with production access.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/1password-hacked-customer-data-and.html
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/26/1password-breach-github-customer-records/
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/1password-breach-exposes-customer-data-internal-source-code/

4. ITALY AND SWITZERLAND FLAG AUTONOMOUS AI ATTACKS
   AI Security · [ai, critical-infrastructure]
   The Swiss and Italian national cyber agencies warned that
   attacks driven by autonomous AI agents are hitting critical
   infrastructure organizations in both countries.
   Why it matters: Agent-driven attacks compress reconnaissance
   and exploitation into minutes, outrunning patch cycles and
   human-speed triage at utilities, transport, and manufacturing
   operators.
   - What to do: Unit 42 tells teams to baseline normal behavior
     and push detection to the edge, since agent-speed
     intrusions leave no room for manual review.
   - What to watch: Cyber insurers now sort clients into
     AI-defense haves and have-nots and say the current policy
     model cannot hold.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/italy-and-switzerland-warn-of-autonomous-ai-driven-cyberattacks/
   - Unit 42 (Palo Alto): https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/ai-threat-horizon/
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/cybersecurity-operations/cyber-insurers-see-new-ai-divide

5. FAKE JOB TESTS HIDE MALICIOUS NUGET PACKAGES
   Software Supply Chain Security · [supply-chain, malware]
   Socket researcher Kush Pandya found 13 malicious NuGet
   packages posing as WinUI 3 and Windows Presentation
   Foundation button controls inside fake take-home job
   assessments.
   Why it matters: Building or running the sample project hands
   attackers access to a .NET developer's machine, and from
   there to source code and credentials.
   - What to do: Treat recruiter-supplied projects as untrusted
     code. Build them in disposable virtual machines and
     restrict developers to vetted package feeds.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/13-malicious-nuget-packages-turn-fake.html
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/threat-intelligence/attackers-net-developers-poisoned-job-assessments

6. PROSECUTORS INDICT RANSOMWARE RECOVERY CO-FOUNDER
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [ransomware, legal]
   A federal grand jury in the District of Rhode Island indicted
   Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, on three wire fraud counts
   for secretly paying ransoms.
   Why it matters: Companies that hire recovery firms may be
   funding the gangs that attacked them, taking on sanctions and
   disclosure exposure they never approved.
   - Zoom in: Agents arrested Palacio Escobar, a Colombian
     national, in Las Vegas on October 22. Each count carries a
     maximum of 20 years in prison.
   - What to do: Require recovery vendors to state in writing
     whether they contact or pay attackers, and keep ransom
     decisions with the general counsel.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/26/ransomware-recovery-fraud-florida-firm-co-founder-charged/
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/ransomware-doj-indictment-recovery

7. ATTACKERS EXPLOIT JUNIPER SESSION SMART CONDUCTOR
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [exploit, patch, network]
   Attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-103406 in Juniper Session
   Smart Conductor to run arbitrary commands as root, and CISA
   added the flaw to its exploited catalog Wednesday.
   Why it matters: Conductor sits at the center of Session Smart
   router deployments, so root command execution there exposes
   the network paths it manages.
   - What to do: Patch Session Smart Conductor now and keep its
     management interface off the public internet. Review
     Conductor hosts for unexpected root processes and new
     accounts.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/critical-juniper-session-smart-conductor-flaw-exploited-in-attacks/

8. SYS010 MALWARE HIJACKS BROWSER ENTERPRISE MANAGEMENT
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, infostealer]
   A new version of the SYS01 infostealer, SYS010, enrolls
   victim browsers into enterprise-style management to
   force-install an extension that steals data.
   Why it matters: The forced extension reads Gmail and Outlook
   mail, turning an ad click into persistent mailbox
   surveillance with no user consent prompt.
   - Zoom in: Operators deliver the malware through Meta ads,
     the same channel earlier SYS01 campaigns used to reach
     business account holders.
   - What to do: Alert on new browser cloud management policies
     and enrollment profiles appearing on endpoints, and block
     extension installs outside an approved list.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/sys010-malware-abuses-browser-management-to-spy-on-gmail-outlook/

9. KEENADU MALWARE SHIPS PREINSTALLED ON ANDROID PHONES
   Software Supply Chain Security · [malware, android,
   supply-chain]
   Kaspersky found the Keenadu malware family preinstalled in
   Android firmware on more than 220,000 devices during the
   first ten months of 2026.
   Why it matters: Buyers cannot remove malware that lives in
   firmware, so these handsets reach employees and customers
   already compromised.
   - By the numbers: Keenadu and the older Triada family account
     for 88% of preinstalled infections, and monthly detections
     grew more than 2.5 times over the past year.
   - What to do: Source Android hardware from vetted suppliers
     and verify firmware images before issuing devices. Keep
     unbranded handsets off corporate networks.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/26/keenadu-malware-android-devices/

10. GOOGLE TIGHTENS RULES FOR WORKSPACE AI AGENTS
   AI Security · [ai, policy, cloud]
   Google will require third-party AI agents to reach Workspace
   data through Model Context Protocol or command-line
   interfaces starting February 2027.
   Why it matters: Agents running on employee credentials carry
   that person's access to company data, and the rule forces an
   inventory of every AI tool touching Workspace.
   - Zoom in: The package adds dedicated agent identities, human
     approval for sensitive operations, and prompt injection
     screening across Google Workspace and Google Cloud.
   - What to do: List the third-party AI tools connected to
     Workspace now and confirm each one supports Model Context
     Protocol or command-line access before February 2027.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/26/google-ai-agent-security-requirements/
