2. AI FRAMEWORKS RUN THE POST-EXPLOITATION PHASE
   AI Security · [ai, ransomware, apt]
   Latest developments: Kaspersky's Securelist published an
   analysis on November 2, 2026 of attacks on educational
   institutions in which the CyberStrikeAI penetration-testing
   framework automated the entire post-exploitation phase;
   Sophos, which tracks the Aur0ra ransomware group as GOLD
   OGRE, recovered Cursor AI coding assistant artifacts from
   late August 2026 showing the operators handing agents VPN
   logins, proxy access, targets, and objectives, after which
   the agents mapped networks, collected credentials, and worked
   up to domain administrator; and Cisco Talos researcher Ryan
   Fetterman named the three models the VOLTA-AUTODOXER stealer
   calls on to rank a victim's data by resale value as Claude,
   Gemini, and Kimi.
   Commodity agentic tooling now does the network mapping,
   credential collection, and privilege escalation that human
   operators used to perform by hand, which compresses the time
   between first access and domain takeover. Security teams
   should treat agent traffic and coding-assistant credentials
   as attacker infrastructure and watch for privilege escalation
   that moves faster than a person can type.

3. GOOGLE DISMANTLES THE XCPE ANDROID BOTNET
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [botnet, android, takedown]
   Latest developments: Google and industry partners seized the
   servers behind XCPE in late October 2026, breaking a botnet
   of more than 1.3 million enrolled Android devices, most of
   them television set-top boxes, and Google's Threat
   Intelligence Group attributes the operation with high
   confidence to Videostrong Technology, the manufacturer that
   built the backdoor into the firmware and sold criminals
   access to its customers' home networks for advertising fraud
   and traffic proxying; Google has banned the company.
   XCPE lived in the firmware of cheap Android TV boxes before a
   buyer ever opened the carton, so owners could neither see it
   nor remove it. Anyone running one of these boxes should
   unplug it from the network, and buyers of low-cost streaming
   hardware should stick to devices certified under Google's
   Play Protect program.
