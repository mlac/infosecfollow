1. META'S MUSE PROFILES THE PEOPLE AROUND YOU
   AI Security · [ai, privacy]
   Latest developments: Wired reported on October 3, 2026 that
   Meta's Muse assistant, which millions of people have
   downloaded, builds detailed profiles of a user's friends and
   family as the price of carrying out their requests, a day
   after Apple rewrote full-disk access permissions to curb what
   AI agents reach over Meta's argument that the permission
   alone does not let Muse read messages, and 404 Media reported
   that human workers handle some of what Muse appears to do on
   its own.
   Muse needs broad device access to act for its user, and that
   access sweeps in contacts, messages, and files describing
   people who never agreed to anything. Review which agents hold
   full-disk access on corporate Macs, and treat an assistant's
   permission grant as a disclosure of everyone in the address
   book.

2. FORTRA BOKS PATCHES CLOSE AN AUTHENTICATION BYPASS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, vulnerability,
   banking]
   Latest developments: Fortra shipped fixes on October 3, 2026
   for critical vulnerabilities in BoKS, its access control
   software for Unix and Linux servers, that carry an attacker
   from authentication bypass to shell command execution and
   memory corruption, and Dark Reading warned on October 2, 2026
   that middleware banks and government agencies run alongside
   SWIFT messaging allows remote code execution that reaches
   hardware-based multifactor authentication.
   BoKS centralizes administrator logins and privilege across
   Unix and Linux estates, so a bypass there hands over the
   accounts that manage everything else. Patch BoKS and the
   SWIFT-adjacent middleware now, and audit administrator
   sessions created before the fix.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/fortra-patches-critical-vulnerabilities-in-boks/

4. BACKDOORS POSE AS ZOOM AND MAIL SECURITY APPLIANCES
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, macos, linux]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek reported on October 2, 2026
   that a counterfeit Zoom installer plants a macOS backdoor
   called CloudSyncD, with the dropper carrying a complete
   universal Mach-O binary inside itself, roughly 756 kilobytes
   in the development build, and extracting it at runtime, and
   Dark Reading described three newly discovered Linux backdoors
   built to look and behave like legitimate Asian mail security

   time on the host. Restrict Mac users to installers from
   zoom.us, and verify the provenance and file hashes of mail
   gateway images before deployment.
