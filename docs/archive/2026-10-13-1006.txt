1. CIRCUIT BOARD UNDER THE LEDGER SCREEN
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [crypto, hardware, supply chain]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek described the implant's
   construction on October 13, 2026: a small circuit board sat
   hidden under the screens of the Ledger signers, read user
   data, and transmitted it to the attackers, who drained
   roughly $86 million from wallets created on devices the
   Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis sold; Ledger says the
   implant captured recovery phrases, and Kim Zetter reported
   the same day that the scheme reached legitimate resellers in
   both Asia and Europe and put the total theft near $100
   million.
   Ledger, the Paris-based hardware wallet maker, has paused
   sales through CryptoBilis and ties every confirmed theft to a
   device from that reseller. Anyone holding a Ledger signer
   bought through a third-party reseller should verify the
   device and move funds to a seed generated on hardware bought
   direct.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/86-million-crypto-heist-linked-to-ledger-signer-hardware-implant/
   - Kim Zetter (Zero Day): https://www.zetter-zeroday.com/who-is-planting-backdoors-in-ledger-crypto-wallets/
   - The Record: https://therecord.media/ledger-cryptocurrency-wallet-implant-theft
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ledger-confirms-hardware-wallet-spy-implant-behind-crypto-thefts/

2. GITLAB PATCHES A 10.0 REGEX COMPILER FLAW
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, zero-day, devops]
   Latest developments: GitLab's maximum-severity bug now
   carries an identifier and a mechanism: CVE-2026-97229 is an
   integer overflow in the CI/CD Regular Expression Compiler, it
   affects both Community Edition and Enterprise Edition, and an
   unauthenticated attacker reaches arbitrary code execution on
   the server through a specially crafted regular expression in
   a CI/CD configuration.
   Self-managed GitLab instances carry the risk; GitLab.com
   customers do not. Administrators should apply the patched
   releases immediately, since the flaw needs no account on the
   target.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/gitlab-patches-cvss-100-flaw-that-could.html
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/13/gitlab-ci-cd-regular-expression-compiler-vulnerability-cve-2026-97229/
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/gitlab-warns-of-max-severity-rce-flaw-in-regex-compiler-engine/

3. FRENCH SUPERMARKET FREEZERS DRIVEN TO 50 DEGREES
   Critical Infrastructure Security · [ics, breach]
   Latest developments: The attack ran hotter than first
   reported: on the night of October 11, 2026 the attackers
   warmed refrigerators and freezers at hundreds of French
   supermarkets to 50 degrees Celsius, retail chains including
   Les Mousquetaires confirmed the losses, and the entry point
   was the single Danfoss controller that runs every cooling
   unit in a store, a device maintenance firms manage remotely.
   One remotely managed controller governs all the refrigeration
   in a store, which turns a single credential into spoiled
   stock across a chain. Grocery operators should inventory
   remote access held by refrigeration maintenance contractors
   and cut any path that reaches controllers from the open
   internet.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attackers-heat-french-supermarket.html
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/refrigeration-controller-hack-spoils-food-across-european-supermarkets/
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/12/france-cyberattack-supermarket-freezers/

6. SAP PATCHES A MAXIMUM-SEVERITY OAUTH2 SSRF
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, ssrf]
   Latest developments: SAP shipped its October 2026 security
   updates on October 13, 2026, fixing 20 flaws across its
   product line, and rated one of them maximum severity: a
   server-side request forgery in the OAuth2 handling of SAP
   Cloud SDK for Python.
   The Cloud SDK for Python underpins custom applications and
   integrations that talk to SAP cloud services, and a
   server-side request forgery turns such an application into a
   relay for an attacker's requests into internal systems. SAP
   customers should work the October patch day notes and update
   affected SDK dependencies in their own builds.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/sap-warns-of-maximum-severity-oauth2-ssrf-security-flaw/

7. NPM TRUSTED PUBLISHING OPENED 1,500 PACKAGES
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [supply chain, patch]
   Latest developments: Bloom Security disclosed on October 13,
   2026 that npm's Trusted Publishing feature let an attacker
   publish new versions of another maintainer's package by
   registering a GitHub repository name the maintainer had
   abandoned, with no stolen credential anywhere in the chain;
   Bloom counted more than 1,500 exposed packages carrying over
   10 million downloads in total, and GitHub, which owns npm,
   says it has shipped fixes.
   Trusted Publishing ties publish rights to a GitHub repository
   instead of a token, so a renamed or deleted repository leaves
   the claim open to whoever registers that name next.
   Maintainers should audit the repositories their npm packages
   trust and reclaim any name they have retired.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/13/slugfest-npm-trusted-publishing-flaw/

8. FRONTIER AI CLEARS EVERY GOAL IN BOOZ ALLEN'S OT LAB
   AI Security · [ai, ics]
   Latest developments: Booz Allen reported on October 13, 2026
   that frontier AI models it set against an isolated industrial
   control testbed in its Chantilly, Virginia lab met all eight
   attack objectives the firm had defined: the models mapped the
   network, matched equipment to known weaknesses, wrote working
   exploits, pivoted into the systems that run production, and
   in the final test found a robotic arm, took administrative
   control, and moved it.
   The test puts a measured floor under claims that
   general-purpose models can run operational technology
   intrusions end to end, against the very equipment that moves
   physical things. Plant operators should assume exploit
   development against their known-vulnerable gear now costs an
   attacker hours instead of weeks and prioritize segmentation
   between business networks and production control.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/13/frontier-ai-ot-security-threat/

9. LG WEBOS CHAIN ROOTS SMART TVS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, iot]
   Latest developments: SSD Secure Disclosure published an
   advisory on October 12, 2026 describing three vulnerabilities
   in LG's webOS that chain into root access on certain smart
   TVs for any attacker on the same local network, with no PIN
   and no action from the owner; researchers ran the chain on
   the 32LQ636BPSA and 27TQ600SW models on webOS 7.5.7-25, and
   LG has issued a fix.
   A rooted television on a corporate or home network gives an
   attacker a persistent listening post with a camera-free but
   microphone-capable foothold behind the firewall. Owners and
   facilities teams should take the LG firmware update and keep
   displays on a segmented network away from business systems.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/lg-smart-tvs-vulnerable-to-pre-auth.html

10. MARKLOGIC FLAWS HAND MANAGERS THE SERVER
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, privilege escalation]
   Latest developments: Progress Software published 31
   vulnerabilities in MarkLogic Server on October 12, 2026 and
   rated five of them critical; two let an account holding only
   partial management privileges seize administrator control of
   the database server and keep it.
   MarkLogic runs enterprise document and search workloads, so
   an escalation from delegated manager to full administrator
   exposes everything the database holds. Progress has shipped
   fixes since September 10, 2026 in MarkLogic 11.3.7 and
   12.1.0, and administrators should upgrade and review who
   holds delegated management rights.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/two-critical-marklogic-flaws-could-give.html
