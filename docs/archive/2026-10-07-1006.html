4. FortiBleed Locks Admins Out of Fortinet Firewalls

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [exploit, credentials]

Latest developments: The FBI and the United States Secret Service issued a joint advisory on Tuesday, October 6, 2026 saying the FortiBleed credential-harvesting campaign against internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways remains active, cited SOCRadar's verified count of 86,644 compromised devices in 194 countries, and warned that victims lose access to their own Fortinet devices when the attacker deletes or alters administrator credentials.

read more

FortiBleed works through reused and leaked credentials plus Fortinet's legacy SHA-256 password storage, which hands attackers working logins on gateways that terminate remote access for whole organizations. Administrators should rotate every account on the appliance, pull credentials that predate the current password scheme, restrict management interfaces, and check administrator lists for deletions and additions.

Latest developments: Google named the three country-code domain registries on Tuesday, October 6, 2026: attackers compromised the third-party operators of .gh for Ghana, .sl for Sierra Leone, and .as for American Samoa, changed the authoritative DNS records, and used that control to obtain HTTPS certificates for several Google domains and for domains belonging to other large organizations.

read more

Control of a registry's DNS puts every domain under that ending at risk, because certificate authorities validate ownership through DNS and will issue to whoever answers.
