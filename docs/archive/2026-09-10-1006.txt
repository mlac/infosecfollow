1. RANSOMWARE CREWS HIT WATCHGUARD, FORTINET, AND CISCO
   FIREWALLS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [ransomware, patch, exploit]
   Latest developments: CISA added three flaws to its Known
   Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and gave federal civilian
   agencies until September 12, 2026 to patch them: the
   maximum-severity Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center
   authentication bypass CVE-2026-20079, the Citrix NetScaler
   authentication bypass CVE-2026-19490, which SecurityWeek
   dates to attacks running since at least September 3, 2026,
   and the Fortinet code execution flaw CVE-2025-25249, which
   attackers use to install a remote access trojan called
   PivotC2; Cisco Talos now tracks a second Secure Firewall
   Management Center bug, CVE-2026-20316, and puts both
   state-sponsored and financially motivated crews behind that
   exploitation; and CISA confirmed that ransomware gangs also
   exploit the critical WatchGuard Firebox remote code execution
   flaw it first flagged in December.
   Firewall and firewall-management products from WatchGuard,
   Fortinet, Citrix, and Cisco carry flaws that attackers
   exploit right now, and all four vendors shipped the fixes
   months ago. Apply the patches and comb every exposed
   appliance for accounts and web shells nobody recognizes.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisa-watchguard-rce-flaw-now-exploited-in-ransomware-attacks/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisa-flags-exploited-cisco-citrix.html

3. FAKE IT CALLS REACH EMPLOYEES' PERSONAL PHONES
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [phishing, identity, cloud]
   Latest developments: Microsoft Security Research said it has
   tracked a campaign since May 2026 in which callers and
   texters pose as internal IT staff, reach employees on their
   personal phones, talk them into handing over access to
   corporate cloud accounts, and then pull files and mail out of
   Microsoft 365, SharePoint, OneDrive, and inboxes for weeks;
   Microsoft Threat Intelligence documented passkey-themed lures
   in the same vein that persuade targets to enroll an
   attacker's authentication method, and Barracuda found
   phishing that routes victims through genuine Microsoft OAuth
   and Teams infrastructure, then assembles the fake login page
   inside the victim's own browser from a blob URL that never
   touches a web server.
   The first contact lands on a personal device, where corporate
   phone, email, and web controls have no visibility. Tell staff
   that IT never calls personal numbers to request codes or
   approvals, and force help desk verification back through an
   internal channel.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/10/microsoft-365-social-engineering-personal-phones/
   - Microsoft Security Blog: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/09/passkey-themed-social-engineering-leads-identity-cloud-compromise/

4. DEFAULT KEYS AND SPOOFED WORKLOADS IN AI PLUMBING
   AI Security · [ai, identity, cloud]
   Latest developments: Wiz Research reported that nearly one in
   ten internet-facing LiteLLM gateways it scanned in February
   2026 accepted sk-1234, the example administrator key printed
   in LiteLLM's own setup guide; Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42
   showed how root access on a single Kubernetes node lets an
   attacker read SPIFFE and SPIRE metadata and impersonate every
   workload identity sharing that node; and Dark Reading
   detailed workflow identity hijacking, which reaches
   enterprise data through an unauthenticated entry point and
   walks past standard controls.
   An AI gateway sits between a company's applications and the
   model providers it pays, and its administrator key opens
   every request, budget, and provider credential behind it.
   Rotate the default keys, pull gateways off the public
   internet, and treat a compromised node as a compromise of
   every workload identity on it.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/nearly-1-in-10-exposed-litellm-gateways.html
   - Unit 42 (Palo Alto): https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/kubernetes-spiffe-spire-identity-spoofing/
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/threat-intelligence/identity-based-ai-attack-security-enterprise-data

9. EU CYBER RESILIENCE ACT SETS A 24-HOUR CLOCK
   Policy and Regulation · [policy, compliance, breach]
   Latest developments: Dark Reading reported that the European
   Union's Cyber Resilience Act reporting duties take effect
   Friday, September 11, 2026, giving any company that sells
   connected products in the European Union 24 hours to notify
   authorities once it discovers a serious product security
   incident.
   The act covers hardware and software with digital elements
   sold in the European Union and obliges manufacturers to tell
   regulators about actively exploited vulnerabilities and
   severe incidents on a one-day clock. Product, legal, and
   incident response teams need a named owner and a filing path
   in place before Friday.
