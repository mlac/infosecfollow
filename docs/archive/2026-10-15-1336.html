1. Spyware Infects Phones Through Ad Networks

Surveillance and Spyware · [spyware, zero-click, surveillance]

Latest developments: The Citizen Lab said on October 15, 2026 that Candiru, the Israeli spyware maker that sells to governments, infected devices at the news network Al Jazeera through ordinary online ads, with the hidden code running the moment an ad loaded and, in some cases, loading out of sight in the background; Wired called the finding the first technical evidence of mercenary spyware traveling through advertising, 404 Media documented that advertisers on OpenAI's ChatGPT can target a user by email address, phone number, or unique phone identifier, and Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the manufacturer of the spyware Poland's previous ruling party used held access to the data Polish operators harvested, calling the affair one of the biggest scandals in Polish history since the fall of communism.

read more

Mercenary spyware vendors sell phone-compromise services to government customers, and targeted advertising gives them a delivery channel that reaches a chosen handset with no message and no link. Executives, journalists, and government personnel who face this threat should keep phones on current operating system versions, turn on lockdown modes where the vendor offers them, and treat ad-blocking at the network edge as a security control.

2. Langflow, Nextcloud, and AhsayCBS Under Live Exploitation

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, exploit, ai]

Latest developments: Previdian warned that attackers exploit CVE-2026-105740, a 9.9-rated authenticated remote code execution flaw in Langflow, the open-source AI application builder IBM acquired through DataStax, and logged 1,423 honeypot exploit attempts; three administrators reported on GitHub that attackers plant web shells on Nextcloud servers through the optional Collabora Online Built-in CODE Server app, with the first logged attack on September 10, 2026; and Ahsay Backup of Singapore shipped new fixes for CVE-2026-105133 and CVE-2026-105134 after its earlier patches proved incomplete, a day after CISA put both bugs in its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog alongside Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS CVE-2026-0322 and Cisco Secure Email Gateway CVE-2026-76519.

read more

All three products sit at the edge of enterprise networks and hand an attacker command execution: Langflow runs AI workflows, the Collabora app renders documents inside Nextcloud, and AhsayCBS manages backups. Patch Langflow and AhsayCBS now, disable or update the Collabora Online Built-in CODE Server app, and search web directories for files that execute commands.

3. Drupal, Siemens, and Rockwell Ship Critical Fixes

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, ics, rce]

Latest developments: The patch wave widened on October 14 and 15, 2026: the Drupal Security Team closed a critical remote code execution flaw in the Field UI module of Drupal core with releases 11.4.10, 11.3.19, and 10.6.19, saying core alone leaves the bug out of reach while several contributed modules open paths to it, at least one requiring no login; F5 released NGINX 1.30.6 and 1.31.7 against five flaws, one of them present in every release since May 2005, among them three memory bugs that may run an attacker's code and a TLS flaw that lets an attacker forge a session and skip client-certificate checks; Next.js shipped an out-of-band update for two critical unauthenticated remote code execution bugs, one in React Server Components and one in librsvg, which it uses through the sharp package to process SVG images; Siemens issued 17 advisories, Rockwell Automation fixed a takeover flaw in its ArmorBlock input/output modules, ABB warned that someone may already probe a flaw in its dynovaPRO product, and CISA published advisories on six bugs in J4Care dcm4che medical imaging software, rated as high as 9.1, that let an attacker delete medical images, forge studies, and reassign patient records, plus three flaws rated 9.8 in Guangzhou Juan IoT Technology's EseeCloud app and camera firmware that hand over control of the IP cameras.

read more

Drupal runs tens of thousands of government and enterprise websites, NGINX fronts a large share of the web, and the no-login Drupal path arrives through add-on modules most sites install. Update Drupal core, NGINX, and Next.js immediately, audit contributed Drupal modules, and schedule the Siemens, Rockwell, ABB, dcm4che, and EseeCloud advisories into the next plant and clinical maintenance window.

6. Fake AI Brands and CAPTCHA Lures Push Commodity RATs

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, phishing, ai]

Latest developments: ANY.RUN tied 1,258 public sandbox analyses between April and October 2026 to campaigns that impersonate popular AI products to spread malware, steal browser sessions, and take over business accounts, with United States companies making up 16% of country-attributed targets; Barracuda found counterfeit CAPTCHA checks in 43% of the phishing kits it examined from 2025, now used to trigger malware downloads and to talk victims into running commands themselves; Group-IB analysts Alpomish Asadov and Razvan Raducu detailed KGB RAT, a Windows trojan built on NjRAT that sells for as little as $150 a month and gives buyers the webcam, microphone, keystrokes, and saved passwords; and SecurityWeek described CikaBika Bot RAT, a phishing-delivered tool that runs its command and control through Discord.

read more

Cheap remote access trojans now arrive behind the brand name of an AI assistant and a fake human-verification box, which gets past user skepticism and past filters that trust Discord traffic. Block downloads of AI client software outside an approved list, teach staff that a real CAPTCHA never asks them to paste a command into Windows Run, and alert on Discord as an outbound channel from servers and workstations.

7. Supermarket Refrigeration Attack Widens to Copeland and Dixell

Critical Infrastructure Security · [ics, retail, exploit]

Latest developments: SecurityWeek reported on October 15, 2026 that the campaign against French grocery refrigeration reaches controllers from Copeland and Dixell as well as Danfoss, that roughly 700 supermarkets lost chilled and frozen stock, and that no group has claimed the attacks; Ars Technica said some overheated units sparked fires, and Dark Reading traced the exposure to outdated firmware on internet-facing controllers that the stores had handed to third-party maintenance contractors.

read more

One refrigeration controller runs every cooling unit in a store, and maintenance firms reach those controllers over the internet to service them, which hands an attacker the whole cold chain. Retailers should inventory every remotely managed building control device, demand firmware versions and patch dates from their service contractors, and put the controllers behind a gateway that requires authentication.

8. GhostAction Drains Secrets From 30,000 Repositories

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [supply-chain, credentials, breach]

Latest developments: GitGuardian measured a sharp escalation of the GhostAction credential-theft campaign on October 8 and 9, 2026: the operators compromised the GitHub accounts of about 1,900 users, reached more than 30,000 repositories belonging to 3,000 users and organizations, and tried to pull 3,174 secrets out of the continuous integration configurations of public repositories.

read more

Development teams should rotate CI secrets, require phishing-resistant multifactor authentication on GitHub accounts, and review recent workflow file changes.

10. PEERSTITCH Implant Hides in FortiGate Firewalls

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [malware, network-edge, exploit]

Latest developments: Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 published an intrusion investigation on October 14, 2026 in which attackers exploited CVE-2024-47575, the FortiManager authentication flaw, reached more than 500 managed firewalls, and deployed PEERSTITCH, memory-resident ARM64 malware that leaves nothing on the appliance's disk.

read more

FortiManager centrally administers FortiGate firewalls, so one compromised manager carries an attacker to every device it controls, and a memory-resident implant evades file-based scanning. Organizations running FortiManager should confirm they applied the CVE-2024-47575 fix Fortinet shipped in 2024, rotate device credentials, and compare running firewall configurations against known-good baselines.
