1. AOMEI Update Servers Shipped a Backdoor

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [supply chain, malware, backdoor]

Latest developments: AOMEI, registered in Hong Kong, confirmed to Recorded Future News on October 20, 2026 that attackers repeatedly altered configuration files on its update servers between April and July 2026, and the company has yet to notify customers; SecurityWeek identified the payload as a downloader for the ShinyRoad modular backdoor framework, delivered through the backup software's update and promotional pop-up mechanism.

read more

AOMEI Backupper, a Windows backup program, periodically fetches instructions from the vendor's own servers, so any copy that checked in during one of the five-to-ten-minute tampering windows downloaded and ran a backdoor. Administrators should hunt for ShinyRoad activity on machines running AOMEI Backupper and keep the software off those hosts until AOMEI accounts for the compromise.

2. ClickFix Turns Signed Siemens Software Into a Loader

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, malware, exploited]

Latest developments: Siemens updated its advisory on October 17, 2026 to point customers to version 4.0.9.8, which closes CVE-2026-110936, the DLL sideloading flaw rated 8.4 under CVSS v4 that Jose Oregon of Huntress Labs reported and that attackers already exploit by pairing a genuine signed copy of Siemens Software Center with malicious DLLs delivered through ClickFix lures; Forescout Research - Vedere Labs disclosed ROMULUS, a ClickFix delivery platform whose fake landing pages push Legion Loader and other malware, and Google began updating Chrome's Enhanced Protection mode to flag scam pages and warn users when one tries to get them to copy a malicious command.

read more

ClickFix tricks a user into pasting an attacker's command into Windows, so the victim runs the malware personally and sidesteps download controls. Industrial customers should update Siemens Software Center at once, and security teams should block clipboard-to-Run-dialog behavior and alert on it.

3. Armies and Vendors Race to Control AI Agents

AI Security · [ai, agents, policy]

Latest developments: Help Net Security reported on October 20, 2026 that the United States Army seeks AI agents that enter an adversary's network, locate a target, copy themselves onto nearby tablets and computers, and delete themselves before anyone notices; Microsoft extended Project Perception with Guardian Agents that tighten an agent's permissions the moment its activity strays from its assigned job, treating agents as insiders; and NIST, CISA, and the National Security Agency laid out ten voluntary practices for preparing for, detecting, responding to, recovering from, and reporting rogue agent incidents.

read more
