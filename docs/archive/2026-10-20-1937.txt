1. EIGHT EMUDHRA INTERMEDIATE CA KEYS COMPROMISED
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [encryption, patch]
   Latest developments: Mozilla and Google's Chrome Root Program
   confirmed on October 20, 2026 that the private keys of eight
   eMudhra intermediate certificate authority certificates sit
   in unknown hands after a researcher reported the exposure;
   Google, Mozilla, Apple, and the Go project blocked the keys
   the same day, Go shipped releases 1.27.3 and 1.26.10 that
   reject any certificate chain passing through them, and nobody
   has yet explained how the keys leaked.
   eMudhra, the Indian certificate authority, operates
   intermediates that sign the certificates websites present, so
   anyone holding one of the eight keys can issue a certificate
   for any server that browsers accept and intercept or
   impersonate traffic. Administrators should take the browser
   updates, rebuild Go programs on 1.27.3 or 1.26.10, and
   reissue any certificate that chains to eMudhra.
   - Ars Technica Security: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/the-internet-goes-on-high-alert-after-discovery-of-8-compromised-tls-certs/
   - Dark Reading: https://www.darkreading.com/cyber-risk/tech-giants-emudhra-certificates-compromised-keys
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/eight-emudhra-ca-keys-compromised.html

2. AOMEI UPDATE SERVERS SHIPPED A BACKDOOR
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [supply chain, malware, backdoor]
   Latest developments: AOMEI, registered in Hong Kong,
   confirmed to Recorded Future News on October 20, 2026 that
   attackers repeatedly altered configuration files on its
   update servers between April and July 2026, and the company
   has yet to notify customers; SecurityWeek identified the
   payload as a downloader for the ShinyRoad modular backdoor
   framework, delivered through the backup software's update and
   promotional pop-up mechanism.
   AOMEI Backupper, a Windows backup program, periodically
   fetches instructions from the vendor's own servers, so any
   copy that checked in during one of the five-to-ten-minute
   tampering windows downloaded and ran a backdoor.
   Administrators should hunt for ShinyRoad activity on machines
   running AOMEI Backupper and keep the software off those hosts
   until AOMEI accounts for the compromise.

3. CLICKFIX TURNS SIGNED SIEMENS SOFTWARE INTO A LOADER
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, malware, exploited]
   Latest developments: Siemens updated its advisory on October
   17, 2026 to point customers to version 4.0.9.8, which closes
   CVE-2026-110936, the DLL sideloading flaw rated 8.4 under
   CVSS v4 that Jose Oregon of Huntress Labs reported and that
   attackers already exploit by pairing a genuine signed copy of
   Siemens Software Center with malicious DLLs delivered through
   ClickFix lures; Forescout Research - Vedere Labs disclosed

   sidesteps download controls. Industrial customers should
   update Siemens Software Center at once, and security teams
   should block clipboard-to-Run-dialog behavior and alert on
   it.
