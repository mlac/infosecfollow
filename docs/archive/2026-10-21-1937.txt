1. PHP2SHELL FORCES AN OUT-OF-CYCLE PHP RELEASE
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch]
   Latest developments: The PHP Foundation explained on October
   21, 2026 that one of the two bugs dates to 2024 and that a
   third-party review by AI companies in August 2026 sharpened
   attention on both, pushing the foundation to publish details
   ahead of its own schedule; the patches landed at 14:00 UTC in
   PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35, Sophos published a
   working proof-of-concept exploit, the SANS Internet Storm
   Center raised its InfoCon to yellow, and Cisco Talos,
   Kaspersky, and Unit 42 shipped Snort rules and detection as
   the first exploitation attempts reached sensors.
   The two flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, which the

   attacker's code on a web server with no login and reach every
   installation that serves web requests, among them PHP-FPM and
   Apache with mod_php. PHP underpins most of the public web,
   including WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, and Magento sites, so
   patch now and hunt for web shells on anything
   internet-facing.

3. EMUDHRA KEYS FELL TO WEAK CRYPTOGRAPHY
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [certificates, patch]
   Latest developments: Cisco Talos published its own advisory
   on October 21, 2026 listing the eight compromised eMudhra
   intermediate certificates for customers who maintain their
   own trust decisions, and Help Net Security supplied the
   timeline: a researcher known as Wayne found the weakness on

8. FINGER COMMAND POWERS NEW CLICKFIX LURES
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [social engineering, malware]
   Latest developments: Dark Reading detailed a ClickFix
   campaign on October 21, 2026 that buys malicious Google
   search results for Amazon queries and calls finger, the
   Windows directory-lookup utility that dates to the early
   internet, to fetch a fake CAPTCHA page that installs malware
   including the Lumma stealer; Push Security told
   BleepingComputer the technique has never run bigger, with
   fake AI installers and trusted chatbot platforms now its most
   convincing lures; and Google began rolling out Chrome
   defenses that use Gemini to analyze suspected scam pages
   server-side, warn users who copy a suspicious command, and
   ask permission before Chrome opens an insecure HTTP
   connection, a prompt now on by default for everyone.
   ClickFix social engineering talks a user into pasting
   attacker-supplied commands into Windows, which sidesteps
   download and email controls entirely. Blocking or alerting on
   finger.exe execution costs nothing in most enterprises and
   removes this delivery path.
