1. NGINX, PLEX, AND FORTIGATE UNDER FIRE
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, zero-day, exploit]
   Latest developments: F5 broke its schedule on October 19,
   2026 and shipped out-of-band patches for several NGINX web
   server flaws, three of them critical bugs that run an
   attacker's code; attackers weaponized the Plex2Shell exploit
   chain against Plex media servers within hours of its
   disclosure, dropping cryptocurrency miners and proxy
   software; and researchers linked the already-exploited
   Fortinet flaw to a stealthy, persistent implant that one
   intruder planted on a FortiGate firewall at a
   telecommunications company.
   All three products sit at the network edge, where NGINX
   fronts much of the web, Plex servers face the internet by
   design, and FortiGate firewalls terminate remote access.
   Patch NGINX and Plex now, and hunt FortiGate devices for the
   implant and for deleted logs rather than trusting a clean
   firmware version alone.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/f5-releases-out-of-band-patches-for-critical-nginx-vulnerabilities/
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/plex2shell-exploit-chain-weaponized-against-plex-servers-hours-after-disclosure/

2. CONNECTWISE SUSPENDS CLOUD SERVICES
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [breach, credentials]
   Latest developments: ConnectWise widened the shutdown on
   October 19, 2026, keeping Cloud Backup, the service it
   formerly sold as SkyKick, along with SaaS Security and Cloud
   Management offline while it finishes remediation and security
   validation.
   Attackers used a compromised credential belonging to the
   ConnectWise Cloud Backup application identity to sign in to
   customer Microsoft 365 tenants, 173 of them by researchers'
   count. Managed service providers and their customers should
   audit Microsoft 365 sign-in logs and application consent
   grants for ConnectWise identities and arrange interim backups
   while the services stay down.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/19/connectwise-cloud-backup-suspended-security-incident/

3. FEDS TIE RANSOM NEGOTIATORS TO SHINYHUNTERS
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [ransomware, extortion]
   Latest developments: Brian Krebs reported on October 19, 2026
   that federal investigators pursuing ShinyHunters, the group
   that recently stole highly sensitive data from the FBI,
   identified executives at two ransomware negotiation firms who
   maintained unusually close relations with the extortion crew,
   and sources close to the investigation say one of them, based
   in the United States, helped ShinyHunters run the website
   that pressured victims into paying.
   ShinyHunters steals corporate data and extorts the owner
   through a public leak site, and victims routinely hire
   negotiation firms to deal with the crew on their behalf. Any
   company that retains a ransom negotiator should establish who
   the firm talks to, how it gets paid, and whether its
   interests sit on both sides of the table.
   - Krebs on Security: https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/10/feds-suspect-ransom-negotiator-ran-shinyhunters-extortion-site/

6. BROADCOM WI-FI FLAW HANDS OVER HOME ROUTERS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch]
   Latest developments: Security researcher Quentin Kaiser
   disclosed on October 16, 2026 an unpatched flaw in Broadcom's
   Wi-Fi setup software that runs code as root on Broadcom-based
   routers from within radio range, demanding neither the
   network password nor any pairing step.
   Kaiser ran the attack against home gateways that three
   Belgian internet providers supply, and in one case read the
   Wi-Fi password straight off the device. Broadcom has shipped
   no fix, so carriers and enterprises running Broadcom-based
   gateways need to press their hardware vendor for patched
   firmware and treat the wireless perimeter as reachable by
   anyone nearby.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/unpatched-broadcom-wi-fi-flaw-lets.html
