1. PAPERCUT SHIPS MAINTENANCE RELEASES AFTER AI-AGENT CAMPAIGN
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, zero-day, ai]

   26.0.5, 25.0.13, and 24.1.10 that replace every emergency
   patch the company had pushed for the two actively exploited
   flaws, and GreyNoise detailed the attacker's method: a
   private lab holding a vulnerable copy of PaperCut NG/MF and

   instances at 395 organizations in 48 countries.

   pair chains an authentication bypass with remote code

   25.0.13, or 24.1.10 and hunt for unfamiliar accounts and
   outbound connections to 45.142.193.132.

5. CISCO FIREWALL FLAWS DELIVER QILIN RANSOMWARE
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [ransomware, patch, zero-day]
   Latest developments: Cisco Talos named the payoff behind the
   three threat clusters exploiting CVE-2026-20079, the
   10.0-rated authentication bypass in the Secure Firewall
   Management Center web interface, and CVE-2026-20316: the
   intruders steal credentials and then deploy Qilin ransomware,
   and the clusters span ransomware crews and state-sponsored
   operators.
   Secure Firewall Management Center sets policy for Cisco
   firewalls, so an unauthenticated bypass there hands an
   attacker the perimeter and the credentials the appliance
   holds. CISA gave federal civilian agencies until September
   12, 2026 to patch; every other operator should install
   Cisco's fixes and rotate stored credentials now.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisco-fmc-flaws-exploited-to-steal.html

9. A FEW DOLLARS KICKS A STRANGER'S PHONE OFF THE NETWORK
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [mobile, research]
   Latest developments: Researchers at Michigan State University
   and three partner schools bought a Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7,
   copied the identification number printed on the sealed box,
   reported the handset to its carrier as lost, and then found
   that the phone, still new and sitting on a lab bench, refused
   to connect, one of six weaknesses they found in how carriers
   process lost-phone reports.
   Anyone who reads a handset's identifier off a box, a receipt,
   or a repair ticket can knock that phone off the cellular
   network for a few dollars, with no access to the device or
   the account. Carriers hold the fix: verify that whoever
   reports a loss actually controls the line.
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/11/cellular-network-lost-phone-reporting/
