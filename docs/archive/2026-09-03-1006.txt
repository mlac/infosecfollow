2. MICROSOFT DETAILS SPRING RING TEAMS IMPERSONATION, NODE.JS
   ABUSED AS LOADER
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [social-engineering, malware]
   Latest developments: Microsoft Threat Intelligence published
   its own technical writeup of a human-operated intrusion
   campaign, matching the Spring Ring operation Unit 42 first
   disclosed, that abuses Microsoft Teams external collaboration
   to impersonate IT support and deploy a Node.js-based implant
   for lateral movement, while Symantec separately documented a
   distinct campaign running since February 2026 that abuses the
   trusted node.exe runtime to deliver payloads against
   government departments, technology companies, and hotels.
   Both campaigns exploit the fact that node.exe carries
   Microsoft's own code-signing trust, letting malicious
   JavaScript payloads slip past defenses tuned to flag
   unfamiliar executables. Organizations should restrict
   external Teams collaboration requests and treat unexpected
   node.exe network activity as suspicious.
   - Microsoft Security Blog: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/02/impersonating-it-support-threat-actors-turn-remote-session-into-enterprise-wide-access/

5. IDSCAN.NET TIED TO 153 MILLION LICENSE SCANS
   Data Breaches · [breach, identity-theft]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek named IDScan.net, the
   Louisiana identity-verification vendor, as the likely source
   of the more than 153 million United States and Canadian
   driver's license images now for sale on a dark-web service,
   and an Ars Technica reporter found their own license in the
   trove within hours of renting a car.
   Businesses scan customer licenses through IDScan.net at
   rental counters, bars, and dispensaries, and the scans
   include the photograph and every field on the card. The FBI's
   New Orleans field office has opened an investigation;
   consumers should freeze credit files and treat license-based
   identity checks as compromised.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/153-million-driver-license-images-offered-on-dark-web/
   - Ars Technica Security: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/my-drivers-license-is-one-of-153-million-for-sale-on-a-new-dark-website/

6. FALCONFLANK ZERO-DAY HITS CROWDSTRIKE FALCON
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, patch, endpoint]
   Latest developments: The researcher known as Chaotic Eclipse,
   also called Nightmare Eclipse, published a proof-of-concept
   for FalconFlank, a privilege-escalation zero-day that abuses
   the malicious-macro remediation routine in the CrowdStrike
   Falcon Sensor, the same researcher whose HardBreacher exploit
   forced a Kaspersky patch on August 31, 2026; Cisco separately
   disclosed that S/MIME flaws in Secure Email remain unpatched
   and shipped fixes for critical IOS XR and Nexus bugs.
   FalconFlank turns a defensive feature of the Falcon endpoint
   agent into a path from a local account to full control of the
   host. The unpatched Cisco Secure Email defects can expose the
   contents of encrypted mail, while the new IOS XR and Nexus
   patches close remote code execution and authentication
   bypass; apply the Cisco updates now and watch for a
   CrowdStrike sensor release.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/researcher-releases-falconflank-poc.html
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-warns-of-unpatched-secure-email-flaws-patches-critical-switch-vulnerabilities/

8. SHAI-HULUD WORM EXPANDS CREDENTIAL-HARVESTING REACH
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [worm, supply-chain, credentials]
   Latest developments: GitGuardian researchers found that a
   recent Shai-Hulud infostealer worm variant now scans 469
   separate credential locations across developer environments,
   CI/CD tooling, cloud configurations, and AI tool configs, up
   from just 189 paths in earlier versions.
   Shai-Hulud is a self-propagating worm that has infected more
   than 1,000 organizations, harvested 500,000 credentials, and
   reached OpenAI's supply chain; authorities charged two
   alleged members of the hacking group TeamPCP over the
   campaign on August 29, 2026. The widened scan list means
   infected machines now leak secrets from AI tool configs and
   CI/CD systems, so teams should rotate credentials well beyond
   traditional password stores.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/shai-huluds-reach-just-grew-to-469.html

9. RMM PHISHING CAMPAIGN SPANS 46 COUNTRIES
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [phishing, rmm]
   Latest developments: ANY.RUN researchers linked 601 cases to
   a remote-monitoring-and-management phishing campaign now
   spread across 46 countries, with the United States the top
   target at 45% of observed activity, after the operation first
   surfaced using fake Canada Revenue Agency tax forms as lures.
   The campaign tricks victims into installing legitimate
   remote-monitoring software, which then hands attackers
   persistent remote access to the compromised machine. Its
   spread from a Canada-focused tax lure to a worldwide
   operation shows how quickly RMM phishing kits get retooled
   for new markets.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/us-becomes-top-target-in-rmm-phishing.html
