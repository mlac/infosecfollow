1. Circuit Board Under the Ledger Screen

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [crypto, hardware, supply chain]

Latest developments: SecurityWeek described the implant's construction on October 13, 2026: a small circuit board sat hidden under the screens of the Ledger signers, read user data, and transmitted it to the attackers, who drained roughly $86 million from wallets created on devices the Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis sold; Ledger says the implant captured recovery phrases, and Kim Zetter reported the same day that the scheme reached legitimate resellers in both Asia and Europe and put the total theft near $100 million.

read more

Ledger, the Paris-based hardware wallet maker, has paused sales through CryptoBilis and ties every confirmed theft to a device from that reseller. Anyone holding a Ledger signer bought through a third-party reseller should verify the device and move funds to a seed generated on hardware bought direct.

3. French Supermarket Freezers Driven to 50 Degrees

Critical Infrastructure Security · [ics, breach]

Latest developments: The attack ran hotter than first reported: on the night of October 11, 2026 the attackers warmed refrigerators and freezers at hundreds of French supermarkets to 50 degrees Celsius, retail chains including Les Mousquetaires confirmed the losses, and the entry point was the single Danfoss controller that runs every cooling unit in a store, a device maintenance firms manage remotely.

read more

One remotely managed controller governs all the refrigeration in a store, which turns a single credential into spoiled stock across a chain. 7. npm Trusted Publishing Opened 1,500 Packages

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [supply chain, patch]

Latest developments: Bloom Security disclosed on October 13, 2026 that npm's Trusted Publishing feature let an attacker publish new versions of another maintainer's package by registering a GitHub repository name the maintainer had abandoned, with no stolen credential anywhere in the chain; Bloom counted more than 1,500 exposed packages carrying over 10 million downloads in total, and GitHub, which owns npm, says it has shipped fixes.

read more

Trusted Publishing ties publish rights to a GitHub repository instead of a token, so a renamed or deleted repository leaves the claim open to whoever registers that name next. Maintainers should audit the repositories their npm packages trust and reclaim any name they have retired.
