1. SPYWARE INFECTS PHONES THROUGH AD NETWORKS
   Surveillance and Spyware · [spyware, zero-click,
   surveillance]
   Latest developments: The Citizen Lab said on October 15, 2026
   that Candiru, the Israeli spyware maker that sells to
   governments, infected devices at the news network Al Jazeera
   through ordinary online ads, with the hidden code running the
   moment an ad loaded and, in some cases, loading out of sight
   in the background; Wired called the finding the first
   technical evidence of mercenary spyware traveling through
   advertising, 404 Media documented that advertisers on
   OpenAI's ChatGPT can target a user by email address, phone
   number, or unique phone identifier, and Prime Minister Donald
   Tusk said the manufacturer of the spyware Poland's previous
   ruling party used held access to the data Polish operators
   harvested, calling the affair one of the biggest scandals in
   Polish history since the fall of communism.
   Mercenary spyware vendors sell phone-compromise services to
   government customers, and targeted advertising gives them a
   delivery channel that reaches a chosen handset with no
   message and no link. Executives, journalists, and government
   personnel who face this threat should keep phones on current
   operating system versions, turn on lockdown modes where the
   vendor offers them, and treat ad-blocking at the network edge
   as a security control.

   Critical Infrastructure Security · [ics, retail, exploit]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek reported on October 15,
   2026 that the campaign against French grocery refrigeration
   reaches controllers from Copeland and Dixell as well as

   dates from their service contractors, and put the controllers

10. PWNESCAPE BREAKS THE WALL BETWEEN GUEST AND HOST
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [linux, cloud, patch]
   Latest developments: The Hacker News reported on October 15,
   2026 that CVE-2026-99999, which researchers nickname
   PwnEscape, sits in the Linux kernel's networking code and
   lets code inside a virtual machine read memory on the host
   computer that runs it; a researcher demonstrated the leak
   against Vercel's live cloud sandboxes, and Vercel confirmed
   it.
   The flaw lives in the kernel itself rather than in one
   provider's configuration, so it reaches any shared Linux host
   that runs untrusted guests, which describes most public cloud
   sandboxes and container platforms. Apply distribution kernel
   updates as they ship, and ask hosting and sandbox providers
   in writing when they patched.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-pwnescape-linux-kernel.html
