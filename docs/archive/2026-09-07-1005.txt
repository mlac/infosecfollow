1. SCREENCONNECT FILE TRANSFERS CARRY MALWARE
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [vulnerability, malware,
   patch]
   Latest developments: Huntress documented three unrelated
   intrusions in which backdoored ScreenConnect instances pushed
   a four-stage Visual Basic Script payload to every newly
   connected host, a worm-like spread that began with a Quick
   Assist tech-support scam, a phishing-delivered MSI installer,
   and a fake update; ConnectWise separately published temporary
   mitigations for the underlying file transfer flaw, confirmed
   it hits both its cloud service and on-premises installations,
   and promised a CVE identifier and a fix within the week.
   ScreenConnect is the remote support and access tool that
   corporate IT departments and managed service providers use to
   take over user desktops, and the flaw abuses the file
   transfer built into live sessions. ConnectWise dated its
   advisory September 3, 2026; administrators should apply the
   interim mitigations now, audit connected clients, and update
   as soon as the patch ships.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/connectwise-warns-of-new-screenconnect-flaw-without-patch/
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/07/connectwise-screenconnect-file-transfer-flaw/
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/rogue-screenconnect-clients-spread-four.html
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/modified-screenconnect-clients-used-in-worm-like-campaign/

2. N-ABLE SHIPS FOURTH N-CENTRAL HOTFIX IN FIVE WEEKS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, zero-day, supply
   chain]
   Latest developments: N-able assigned the maximum-severity
   pre-authentication remote code execution flaw CVE-2026-86218
   and released Hotfix 4 for N-central 2026.3 on September 5,
   2026, its fourth hotfix for the platform in five weeks, and
   told every on-premises operator running a build below
   2026.3.1.14 to install it, including anyone who applied
   Hotfix 3 a day earlier.
   N-central is the remote monitoring and management platform
   managed service providers use to administer customer
   machines, so one compromised server exposes every network
   beneath it. N-able's incident notice says attackers have
   exploited the flaw in the wild while its release notes call
   that unconfirmed; operators should patch immediately and hunt
   for intrusion.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/n-able-issues-fourth-n-central-hotfix.html
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/n-able-patches-max-severity-n-central-flaw-amid-ongoing-attacks/
   - Help Net Security: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/07/n-able-n-central-hotfix-cve-2026-86218/

3. MATHSPACE, BERLIN, AND TREZOR REPORT FRESH DATA THEFT
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [breach, ransomware]
   Latest developments: Mathspace, the online maths learning
   platform, disclosed over the weekend that attackers breached
   its Metabase internal reporting system and stole data on more
   than 1 million students, staff, and parents; Berlin's
   government said a second trove of stolen city data appeared
   online, with hackers publishing login credentials, while
   Germany's Federal Office for Information Security warned
   separately about the Rhysida cybercrime group; and Trezor put
   the total from the August breach at its fulfillment provider
   ShipMonk at 81,000 customers after adding 67,000 in the
   United States.
   All three disclosures grew from intrusions at systems that
   sit behind the primary product—an internal analytics tool,
   city agency accounts, and a third-party shipping vendor.
   Customers and residents should treat exposed names,
   addresses, and credentials as material for targeted phishing,
   and reset any password reused elsewhere.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/mathspace-discloses-data-breach-affecting-over-1-million-people/

5. MIKROTIK ROUTEROS FLAWS CHAINED TO HIJACK ROUTERS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [exploitation, patch, network
   devices]
   Latest developments: BleepingComputer reported that attackers
   now chain two recently disclosed RouterOS vulnerabilities,
   rather than firing the SSH authentication bypass alone, to
   seize full control of MikroTik routers whose SSH service
   faces the internet.
   MikroTik patched the SSH authentication bypass late last week
   after exploitation had already started, and the SANS Internet
   Storm Center told administrators to assume compromise because
   attackers add new accounts to the devices to survive the
   update. Anyone running RouterOS with SSH exposed should
   patch, audit the user list, and rotate credentials.
   - BleepingComputer: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-new-mikrotik-routeros-flaws-to-hijack-routers/
   - SANS Internet Storm Center: https://isc.sans.edu/diary/rss/33314

6. NIGHTMARE ECLIPSE DROPS CROWDSTRIKE, NVIDIA, AND AVAST
   ZERO-DAYS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-day, privilege
   escalation, exploit]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek reported that the
   researcher known as Nightmare Eclipse widened the release
   beyond the CrowdStrike Falcon Sensor bug published last week,
   dropping proof-of-concept exploits for unpatched
   privilege-escalation flaws in Nvidia and Avast software that
   each spawn a shell with SYSTEM privileges.
   Nightmare Eclipse, also called Chaotic Eclipse, has now hit
   four security and driver vendors in eight days, following the
   HardBreacher exploit that forced a Kaspersky patch on August
   31, 2026, and the FalconFlank exploit against CrowdStrike's
   macro-remediation routine. Endpoint software runs with the
   highest privileges on a machine, so defenders should watch
   vendor advisories for fixes and monitor for local escalation
   attempts.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/nightmare-eclipse-drops-crowdstrike-nvidia-avast-zero-day-exploits/

7. NORTH KOREAN CREWS BACKDOOR HAPROXY IN SOUTH KOREA
   Nation-State Activity · [apt, espionage, linux]
   Latest developments: SecurityWeek tied the previously
   undocumented Linux toolkit that hides inside trojanized
   HAProxy load balancers to North Korean hackers, and named
   automotive and media organizations in South Korea as the
   targets of the long-term surveillance operation.
   The toolkit, whose own debug strings call it ted, compiles a
   backdoor directly into the HAProxy binary, so the load
   balancer that fronts a web application also intercepts its
   traffic and serves altered pages to chosen visitors. Anyone
   running HAProxy in the region should verify binary integrity
   against vendor builds and review outbound connections from
   load balancers.
   - SecurityWeek: https://www.securityweek.com/north-korean-hackers-deploy-new-linux-espionage-toolkit/

8. JSCEAL STEALS SESSION COOKIES TO WALK PAST GOOGLE LOGINS
   Ransomware and Cybercrime · [malware, credential theft]
   Latest developments: Check Point Research published a
   teardown of JSCeal, malware compiled to V8 JavaScript
   bytecode that harvests credentials, watches the user,
   intercepts traffic, and replays stolen session cookies to
   enter Google accounts without triggering authentication.
   JSCeal's operators wrap the payloads in
   javascript-obfuscator, protecting strings with RC4 and
   flattening control flow through proxy functions and operation
   wrappers to frustrate analysis. Defenders should treat stolen
   browser session cookies as a credential and shorten session
   lifetimes on Google accounts.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/jsceal-malware-can-bypass-google.html

9. LENOVO ID HOLE OPENED 5,000 DROPBOX ACCOUNTS
   Identity and Access Management · [breach, authentication]
   Latest developments: Graham Cluley, writing on Bitdefender's
   Hot for Security blog, detailed a flaw in Lenovo's login
   system that let hackers into roughly 5,000 Dropbox accounts
   that owners had linked to a Lenovo ID.
   Lenovo laptop owners who connected Dropbox to their Lenovo ID
   for single-sign-on convenience inherited the weakness in
   Lenovo's authentication. Anyone who made that link should
   unlink the accounts, change the Dropbox password, and review
   recent file access.
   - Graham Cluley: https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/lenovo-login-system-hackers-dropbox
