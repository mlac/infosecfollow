1. Record Patch Tuesday Fixes Two Exploited Zero-Days

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, zero-day, windows]

Latest developments: Microsoft shipped fixes on September 8, 2026 for 973 vulnerabilities by the SANS Internet Storm Center's count and 974 by Krebs on Security's, far past the previous high of 664 set in July 2026, among them 113 the company rates critical, 20 it calls potentially wormable, and two privilege-escalation zero-days attackers already exploit: CVE-2026-81963, a Windows link-following flaw, and CVE-2026-85880, a heap-based buffer overflow, both of which CISA added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day.

6. Help Desk Vishing Drains Executive Microsoft 365 Accounts

Ransomware and Cybercrime · [phishing, extortion, identity]

Latest developments: Arctic Wolf gave the data theft and extortion cluster a name, PREY-0058, and said its tradecraft matches the group Google Threat Intelligence Group tracks as UNC6671, tying together the vishing calls, adversary-in-the-middle token theft, and residential-proxy sign-ins already documented against Microsoft 365 tenants.

read more

The operators phone corporate IT help desks, talk their way into a password reset or a session token, then sign in through residential proxies that look like ordinary employee traffic, singling out directors, vice presidents, and other executives; the phishing-as-a-service framework BigBear 2.0 ran the same play past multi-factor authentication at 258 organizations for more than 5,000 Microsoft 365 credentials. 7. Planted Prompts Turn ChatGPT Against Its User

AI Security · [ai, prompt injection, data exfiltration]

Latest developments: Check Point Research published a proof of concept in which one instruction planted in a ChatGPT conversation made the assistant read the user's connected Gmail account and pass the contents to a second ChatGPT account over a hidden channel while it answered the user's actual question normally.

9. WeChat Zero-Click Worm Hijacks Accounts Through a Call

Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [zero-click, vulnerability, mobile]

Latest developments: Researchers at the security company Calif disclosed WeWorm, a worm they built on a critical WeChat flaw that seizes a victim's account through an incoming call the target never answers or touches, then mines that account's saved contacts to call the next set of victims; Calif reported the bug to Tencent in July 2026 and demonstrated the spread across three test phones.

read more

WeChat, Tencent's messaging app, carries the flaw on both iPhone and Android, and the attacker's number must already sit in the target's contact list, a condition each new infection satisfies for the next hop. Calif estimates a live worm could reach millions of devices within hours, so users should install the current WeChat build.
