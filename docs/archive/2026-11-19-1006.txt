1. NGINX2ROOT EXPLOITS GO PUBLIC AS SPLUNK PATCHES 140 BUGS
   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [patch, exploit, end-of-life]
   Latest developments: F5 patched five nginx2root
   vulnerabilities that let remote, unauthenticated attackers
   run arbitrary code, and proof-of-concept exploits for them
   reached the public, with researchers chaining two of the bugs
   to a Linux kernel flaw to take root; Splunk fixed nearly 140
   flaws across three core products, among them authentication
   bypass, remote code execution, and privilege escalation; and
   SonicWall told customers in a Wednesday advisory to replace
   every Gen 6 and Gen 6.5 appliance and pull it off the
   internet edge, because SonicOS 6.x now receives no firmware
   or security updates regardless of severity.
   The nginx2root bugs have sat in NGINX's open-source HTTP/2
   code since version 1.9.5 shipped on September 22, 2015, and
   F5 fixed them in 1.31.9 and 1.30.7. Any internet-facing NGINX
   with HTTP/2 enabled, Splunk deployment, or SonicWall Gen 6
   firewall needs action this week.

   Vulnerabilities and Exploits · [exploit, patch, zero-day]

10. FLASH CHIP ATTACK BREAKS TPM LOCKOUT
   Platform Security · [hardware, encryption]
   Latest developments: Researchers at Technische Universität
   Berlin showed that anyone who opens the case of an AMD-based
   PC, and of some Intel PCs, can reset the TPM lockout that
   throttles PIN guessing, and in lab tests they recovered a
   four-digit Windows Hello PIN on an AMD laptop and a six-digit
   BitLocker PIN on an Intel laptop in days.
   Short Windows Hello and BitLocker PINs rely on the TPM
   refusing repeated guesses, and physical access to the flash
   chip removes that limit. Organizations issuing laptops should
   require long PINs or pre-boot passphrases and treat a lost
   machine as a machine an attacker can open.
   - The Hacker News: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/flash-chip-attack-bypasses-tpm-lockout.html
